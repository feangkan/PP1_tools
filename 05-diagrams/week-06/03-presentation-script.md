# 03 — Presentation script: Week 06 — The Entrepreneur + The Collective

- **Paired diagram:** `01-diagram.md`
- **Time target:** 3–4 minutes
- **Tone:** clear, spoken, not essay language

## Hook (15–20 sec)

"This week had two lectures pulling in opposite directions — Helen on the profession as a collective, and Gwyl Jahn as the entrepreneur. My diagram puts them in one frame, because they're actually asking the same question: innovation shortens how long it takes to make something — so who keeps the benefit?"

## Walk the diagram (100–150 sec)

"Start in the centre — the production cycle. Helen's definition of innovation is simple: *any shortening of the production process*. You design something, you spot an inefficiency, research closes that gap, and the next time it takes fewer parts and less time. That loop is neutral — it's just engineering."

"It stops being neutral the moment you go right, to *where the benefit goes*. Same innovation, three destinations: a balanced price; the collective — where it's shared, taught, and becomes standard practice for everyone; or the entrepreneur — where it's locked up as intellectual property and the savings don't get passed on. Only that third one, Helen argues, contradicts the code of conduct."

"Below that is the machinery that makes the collective model actually work — an eight-step spine from one architect noticing a problem, to sharing it outside their practice, to debate, publication, the institutions adopting it, and finally it becoming part of the codes. That's what 'the profession learning' concretely means."

"The red cluster on the left is that machinery being disabled — Standards Australia sold off so the standards are now behind a paywall, Revit and BIM licences you have to rent, certification schemes you pay into. Helen's phrase is 'the black box' — when you can't see inside the tool, you're a user, not an author."

"And AI, bottom right, is drawn last on purpose — it's every one of these questions in the present tense. It shortens production, so whose benefit is the ten-hours-to-one-hour saving? It gives you an answer without the knowledge to judge that answer. And who checks it — you, or the profession?"

## Critical takeaway (30–40 sec)

"My position: the profession already has a working system for turning a private discovery into shared knowledge — and it's being quietly switched off from three sides at once: privatised standards, rented tools, and now AI, where the benefit and the responsibility both default to the individual and the platform. A manifesto's job is to say which parts of that system we defend as they are, and which parts we rebuild."

## Close (10–15 sec)

"That's the raw material for my Assessment 2 — the maintain points defend the collective machinery, the change points repair it for the AI era. Happy to take questions."

## Backup answers (if asked)
