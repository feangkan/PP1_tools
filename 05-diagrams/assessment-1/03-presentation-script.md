# 03 — Presentation script: Assessment 1 — ARCHITECTLAND

- **Paired diagram:** `01-diagram.md`
- **Use:** Week 5 Monday verbal presentation — this is longer than you'll have time for; cut to your actual slot before presenting
- **Length as written:** ~2.5 minutes spoken

---

**Opening:**

"I mapped the Disciplinary Matrix as a theme park. Here's why: a subway map treats every line as equally important, and my tutor was right that it read flat. A theme park has a hierarchy — one flagship ride everyone queues for, themed lands you can visit in any order, and a back-of-house layer visitors never see. That hierarchy of visibility is my actual argument, not just my visual style."

**Registration Mountain:**

"The one compulsory ride is Registration Mountain — Victoria's real path to the title Architect. Five gated stations: accredited education, logged supervised experience, the APE's exam and interview, ARBV registration, then CPD and insurance on a loop. Fail a gate and you re-queue — resit the exam, log more hours. Only a serious breach gets you ejected — a Tribunal finding, the discipline register. This ride is heavily, deliberately gated."

**The lands:**

"Around it sit the required topics as themed lands — education, employment, legislation, professional bodies, ethics and public value — each connected by a monorail of relationships: enables, limits, depends upon. Two lands matter most for my argument. Innovation Pavilion carries Week 5's pre-reading: Ann Lui's research shows building codes actually get amended through activism — gender-inclusive bathrooms, post-Triangle-Fire fire egress rules, disability access — not just expert committee. And Procurement Annex is new — Weeks 1 to 4 barely mention procurement, so I built it from the AIA's own research: their 2019 survey of 266 practices found novated design-and-construct can strip a *registered* architect of design control, without touching their registration at all."

**Control Booth — the critical position:**

"The smallest thing on my map is the Control Booth, and that's deliberate. It shows a small, overlapping group of people sitting across schools, the Institute and the registration board at once — that's sourced from Week 4. My argument is that the profession polices the one gate everyone can see — who becomes an architect — far more tightly than it polices the two gates that actually decide what gets built: who writes the code, and who keeps design control after the project leaves the drawing board. That mismatch is where I think the Disciplinary Matrix actually needs to change."

**Close:**

"So the critical position isn't 'architects need more power' in a general sense — it's that the profession's own gatekeeping energy is currently pointed at the wrong gate."
