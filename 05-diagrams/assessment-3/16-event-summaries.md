## The story in one paragraph

The NSW Government ran a competition in 1955–57 and Jørn Utzon won it. The Premier, Joe Cahill, wanted building to start quickly, so the podium went up from March 1959 while the roof was still an unsolved design problem. When the roof was finally worked out (late 1961 to January 1962), it was heavier than the podium had been designed for, so columns already built had to be strengthened and extra piers added. That is our Scenario 1: a variation, one extra month and $5,000. The podium finished two years late in early 1963, and the roof shells followed (1963–67). A new government in 1965 took control of payments, and a dispute over fees and plywood prototypes ended with Utzon resigning in February 1966. A new team (Hall, Todd and Littlemore) finished the interiors, the building opened on 20 October 1973, and the architects' completion certificate was issued on 31 May 1975. It cost $102 million against a first estimate of £3.5 million.

## Phase 1 — Client brief to Tender (1955–1958)

### Competition announced — 13 Sep 1955
Premier Cahill announces an international competition for an opera house at Bennelong Point. The brief asks for two halls, one for 3,000–3,500 people and one for 1,200.
**Why it matters:** this is the client's original brief. The large hall was meant to be multi-purpose, which later causes trouble.
*Source: D*

### Competition design phase — 1956
Entrants prepare and submit their designs through 1956.
**Why it matters:** this is the first row in our Register and the start of the Design timeline.
*Source: D, I; Register*

### Utzon wins — 29 Jan 1957
Utzon's entry beats 233 others from 32 countries and takes the £5,000 first prize. The government then commissions him to prepare final drawings and supervise construction.
**Why it matters:** this is the moment the architect is chosen, which leads to the introductory letter and the CAA.
*Source: D, I*

### Minister for Public Works becomes the constructing authority — from 1 Jan 1957
A 1960 Act later confirms Utzon's engagement and every earlier contract, and names the Minister for Public Works as the authority building the Opera House, backdated to 1 January 1957.
**Why it matters:** in our contracts the **Owner / Client is the Minister for Public Works (NSW)**. The real Stage 2 and 3 agreements are signed by the Minister too (C, E).
*Source: D*

### Introductory letter — early 1957 (date not fixed)
The architect writes to the client proposing how the engagement will work, with the draft agreement enclosed.
**Why it matters:** this is Lead 1's first required document. The Register has no date for it, so the group must pick one before 21 Feb 1957.
*Source: Register*

### Client Architect Agreement signed — 21 Feb 1957
The architect is formally appointed and the scope of services and fee are agreed.
**Why it matters:** this is a required document. The Register calls it "CAA 2019", but the brief requires the **2024** edition.
*Source: Register*

### Utzon visits Sydney — Jul 1957
Utzon comes to Sydney to discuss the proposed building with the client.
**Why it matters:** this is the first face-to-face briefing, the kind of client meeting the CAA's concept-design stage records.
*Source: D*

### Engineers engaged — 1957
Ove Arup & Partners (London) become the structural engineers and stay on the project to the end.
**Why it matters:** the sources disagree. D says Utzon engaged them; the brief's project information says the client did. That decides how you fill in CAA Schedule B Item 4 and the E.1 point in Part B. Ask the tutor.
*Source: D; brief*

### Red Book — Mar 1958
Utzon sends the government a full set of plans, sections and elevations. The roofs are now higher and more pointed, but still not buildable.
**Why it matters:** this is the end of concept design, the first approval gate on our board.
*Source: B*

### Tram depot demolished — from Aug 1958
The old Fort Macquarie tram depot on Bennelong Point comes down to clear the site.
**Why it matters:** site preparation before the contractor takes possession.
*Source: D*

## Phase 2 — Tender to Start of construction (1958–1959)

### Stage I tender documents — Dec 1958
The conditions of contract and bills of provisional quantities for Stage I (excavation, concrete, drainage and so on) are prepared.
**Why it matters:** this is the real date for our "Tender documents issued" row (the board had assumed Jul 1958).
*Source: C*

### Construction contract signed — 19 Feb 1959
Stage I is let to Civil & Civic. In our file this is the Simple Works Contract 2018.
**Why it matters:** a required document, and Lead 2's main row. No source gives the real award date, so this is the Register's.
*Source: Register (D confirms Civil & Civic did Stage I)*

### Construction starts — 2 Mar 1959
A ceremony in the rain marks the start of work on the foundations and the podium. The ground turns out to be loose, water-soaked alluvial deposits, so about 700 concrete shafts have to be bored down. The roof's weight is still unknown.
**Why it matters:** this confirms our site possession date. It is also the key fact for Part B: **building started before the design was finished**, because Premier Cahill was in a hurry. Source A itself says it would have been more prudent to solve the design first.
*Source: A, D*

## Phase 3 — Construction to Completion (1959–1975)

### Premier Cahill dies — 19 Oct 1959
Cahill dies in office, seven months after construction began.
**Why it matters:** the client's representative changes, which is the CAA C.3 row Lead 1 is adding. The tutor simplifies the next Premier to Askin; historically Heffron came next (not in sources A–J).
*Source: D (A)*

### Roof still unsolved — 1958 to late 1961
The shells go through parabolic, then ellipsoid, then spherical geometry. By late 1961 there is still no way to build them.
**Why it matters:** this bar runs **over** Stage I construction on our board. The podium is being built for a roof nobody can yet weigh.
*Source: B*

### ★ The piers problem — 1961
Once the roof is split into separate structures and its geometry settles, it is heavier than the podium columns were designed for. Columns already built need strengthening and extra piers under them.
**Why it matters:** this is Scenario 1's trigger. **No source A–J dates it**; it comes from the tutor's video (from Murray's *Saga*). Source B suggests the loads were only fixed in late 1961 to Jan 1962, so a 1962 date is also defensible.
*Source: tutor; Register (1961)*

### ★ Architect tells the client about extra services — 1961 (our assumption)
The redesign is extra work for the architect, so under CAA clause D.8 the architect must tell the client in writing how it affects the fee.
**Why it matters:** this is the client-side twin of SW-12 and a new row for Lead 1. It is not in the Register yet.
*Source: our assumption (CAA D.8)*

### ★ Instruction for a Variation (SW-12) to Adjustment of time (SW-14) — 1961
The architect instructs the contractor to strengthen the columns and add piers. The contractor claims one month and $5,000, and the architect grants the extra month.
**Why it matters:** required forms, Prani Patton's rows (checked by Yu-Chun Ho). The figures are the tutor's worked example.
*Source: Register; tutor*

### Yellow Book — Jan 1962
Utzon submits 38 pages of shell drawings: the spherical solution. The roof is finally buildable.
**Why it matters:** this ends the "roof unresolved" bar and fixes the loads the piers had to carry.
*Source: B*

### Cost plan — 1962
The estimate reaches £13.7 million, which is about A$27.4 million after decimal currency (£1 = $2).
**Why it matters:** this matches the Register's cost plan row (CP-06, A$27.5m), so cite G for it.
*Source: G; Register*

### ★ Contract Price Adjustment (SW-11) — 1962
The architect increases the contract price by $5,000 for the piers.
**Why it matters:** a required form. The final certificate must refer back to this number.
*Source: Register; tutor*

### Stage II agreement — 18 Oct 1962
The Minister for Public Works signs with M.R. Hornibrook (NSW) Pty Ltd to build the roof shells.
**Why it matters:** it shows the real client signatory and the second builder. The Register says "Stage II commences 1962", but that was the contract, not the works.
*Source: E, C*

### Stage I finished — Feb / Mar 1963
The podium is complete, two years behind schedule.
**Why it matters:** this is the actual finish against the planned one (26 Jan 1963 on our baseline). Our single month of SW-14 is only a small part of that two-year delay.
*Source: A (Feb), D (Mar)*

### Stage II starts — Apr 1963
Hornibrook begins the roof shells.
**Why it matters:** this is the correct start for the Stage II bar.
*Source: D*

### Government changes the Major Hall seating — 1963
The client instructs changes to the main hall's seating.
**Why it matters:** an early client-driven change to the brief. It is not our scenario, but it is useful context for Part B.
*Source: G*

### New government takes control — May 1965
The Askin government takes office. Davis Hughes, Minister for Public Works, takes control of payments from the Opera House Committee.
**Why it matters:** the client's decision-maker changes again, and the fee dispute begins.
*Source: D, G*

### Plywood prototypes and withheld fees — end 1965
Utzon needs £60,000 to build plywood-beam prototypes for the interiors; the Minister holds back payments.
**Why it matters:** this shows how a client–architect relationship breaks down (CAA fees, suspension and termination clauses).
*Source: G*

### Utzon resigns — 28 Feb 1966
After a short meeting with Hughes, Utzon sends his resignation that day. Protest marches follow.
**Why it matters:** the CAA ends. If SW-31 is dated after this, the architect who issues it is no longer Utzon, so the group must decide how to handle that.
*Source: D, G*

### New architects appointed — 19 Apr 1966
Peter Hall (design), Lionel Todd (documents) and David Littlemore (supervision), with Government Architect E.H. Farmer, take over.
**Why it matters:** these are the architects for Stage III and the final certificate.
*Source: D, G*

### Utzon leaves Australia — 28 Apr 1966
Utzon and his family depart.
*Source: D, G*

### Drawings bought from Utzon — 17 May 1966
The government pays $160,000 for 130 of Utzon's Stage III drawings.
**Why it matters:** a copyright and intellectual-property point for Part B (CAA F.1: the architect keeps copyright).
*Source: D*

### Stage III agreement — Nov 1966
The Minister signs with Hornibrook for the interiors and completion.
*Source: C*

### Review of Program — 12 Dec 1966
The new architects propose major changes to how the halls are used (the Major Hall becomes concert-only).
**Why it matters:** a client and architect change to the brief. It is not our scenario, but it shows the brief changing mid-construction.
*Source: D (F)*

### Last shell placed — 17 Jan 1967
The 2,194th and final shell segment is installed. Stage II is effectively complete and interior work can begin. (D gives Stage II completion as Mar 1967.)
*Source: F (D)*

### White Book — 1967–69
Hall, Todd & Littlemore's first designs for the Concert Hall, Opera Theatre and glass walls.
*Source: H*

### Stage III working drawings — Jul 1968
The working drawings for Stage III are dated (bound as a volume in Jun 1969).
**Why it matters:** the real date for the Register's "Stage 3 contract drawings, 1968" row.
*Source: I*

### Glass-wall prototype specification — Jan 1969
Hall, Todd & Littlemore specify a prototype for the glass walls; more than 6,200 m² of glass is ordered from France.
*Source: C (F)*

### First test concert — 17 Dec 1972
The Sydney Symphony Orchestra plays the first test concert in the Concert Hall.
*Source: D*

### Official opening — 20 Oct 1973
The Queen opens the Opera House, followed by a two-week festival.
**Why it matters:** this is our practical completion certificate, the end of the construction stage.
*Source: J, D*

### Final cost — May 1974
The final cost is $102 million (excluding the organ and car park).
**Why it matters:** this matches the tutor's figure, so use it and not the Register sample's "107M".
*Source: D*

### Architects' Certificate of Completion — 31 May 1975
The architects certify completion.
**Why it matters:** a real, sourced date for our **SW-31 Final Certificate**. It also fits the contract order: practical completion (Oct 1973), then 12 months of defects liability, then the final certificate. The Register says 1974; update it if the group agrees.
*Source: D*
