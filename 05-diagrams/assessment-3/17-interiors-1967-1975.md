## What happened: a summary of each interior event

### The story of 1967–1975 in one paragraph
With the last roof segment in place in January 1967, the shells were finished but empty, and the new architects had inherited sketches rather than working drawings. In March 1967 the government approved their changed brief: the big hall became a concert hall only, opera moved to the smaller hall, and the stage machinery already built for opera was scrapped over the engineer's objection. Hall, Todd & Littlemore then designed the interiors from scratch (the White Book, 1967–69) and site work ran from 1968. The glass walls came first, redesigned around steel mullions and finished by 1971. The Concert Hall's acoustics were tuned with 21 acrylic reflectors in 1972 and tested at a concert for the builders in December 1972. The theatres opened with *War and Peace* on 28 September 1973, the Queen opened the building on 20 October 1973, and the architects certified completion on 31 May 1975. The Grand Organ was left out of the main works and finished in 1979, at three times its budget.

### Interiors can begin — 17 Jan 1967
The last of 2,194 roof segments goes in, ending Stage II. The contract for Stage III (Nov 1966) is already signed with Hornibrook.
**Why it matters:** the Contract timeline hands over from the roof builder's stage to the interiors.
*Source: F, C*

### The brief changes — Mar 1967
Cabinet approves Hall's recommendation: a 2,800-seat concert hall and a 1,500-seat opera theatre. The Major Hall is no longer multi-purpose.
**Why it matters:** the client changes the brief after construction has started. It isn't our scenario, but it is the biggest scope change of the whole project and a good Part B comparison with Scenario 1.
*Source: R, M*

### The engineer objects; stage machinery scrapped — 28 Mar 1967
Ove Arup writes to Minister Hughes that abandoning opera in the Major Hall undermines the reason the shells are shaped as they are. The stage machinery already made for opera, and partly installed, is thrown away.
**Why it matters:** a consultant's professional advice given in writing, and wasted work paid for by the client. In contract terms that would be an omission variation.
*Source: S*

### Waterproofing specification — Mar 1967
The specification for waterproofing measures and membranes is issued.
**Why it matters:** one of the first Stage III technical documents, and an example of a specification row for a register.
*Source: C*

### The White Book — 1967–69
Hall, Todd & Littlemore's first designs: sections through the Concert Hall and Opera Theatre, and the design principles for the glass walls.
**Why it matters:** this is where the new team's own design starts. Copyright in these designs sits with the new architects (CAA F.1), separate from Utzon's.
*Source: H*

### Glass walls redesigned in steel — 1968
Hall designs the glass walls on a steel-mullion frame, reinterpreting Utzon's plywood concept. More than 6,200 m² of glass is ordered from France.
**Why it matters:** a material substitution (plywood to steel) with quality and cost consequences, much like Scenario 3 in the brief.
*Source: L, F*

### Stage III on site — 1968–1973
Interior and glass-wall construction runs for about five years: the Concert Hall is under construction in 1969; glass-wall formwork, Minor Hall backstage and the Drama Studio in 1970; the glass walls are nearly finished in 1971; the Northern Foyer is completed in 1972.
**Why it matters:** this is the actual interior construction period on our Contract timeline.
*Source: K*

### Stage III working drawings — Jul 1968
The construction drawings for Stage III are dated, and later bound into a volume in June 1969.
**Why it matters:** this dates the Register's "Stage 3 contract drawings, 1968" row.
*Source: I*

### Glass-wall prototype specification — Jan 1969
Hall, Todd & Littlemore specify a prototype to test the glass-wall design before it is built in full.
**Why it matters:** a quality-assurance step: test a mock-up first. It links to the AACA competency on site quality (PC54).
*Source: C*

### Grand Organ designed — 1969
Organ builder Ronald Sharp reports on the Concert Hall organ: specification, plan and sections.
**Why it matters:** the organ becomes its own contract, outside the main works.
*Source: P*

### Specialist contracts — Aug 1969 and Mar 1970
The architects sign with GEC-Philips for the light fittings (Aug 1969). Hornibrook subcontracts the electronic services to AWA (Mar 1970).
**Why it matters:** these show how a large interior fit-out is split into packages. In our ABIC terms they would be prime-cost items or nominated subcontractors (clause K).
*Source: C*

### Glass walls near complete — 1971
The glass walls at the ends of the shells are almost finished.
*Source: K*

### Acoustic reflectors designed — Dec 1971
Acoustician Jordan and Hall fix the final shape of the Concert Hall reflectors: acrylic rings hung over the stage, because the ceiling is too high for the orchestra to hear itself.
**Why it matters:** a late design change made for quality, close to completion.
*Source: M*

### Reflectors made and installed — Mar–Sep 1972
Sydney firm K.F. Plastics makes 21 reflectors, installed in time for the Minister's site visit on 5 Sep 1972, then adjusted.
**Why it matters:** a specialist package with a hard deadline, installed and then tuned. A useful example of instruct to install to test.
*Source: M*

### Opera Theatre stage machinery — Dec 1972
Waagner-Biro's second volume of assembly drawings covers the machinery for the new Opera Theatre, replacing what was scrapped from the Major Hall.
*Source: T*

### First test concert — 17 Dec 1972
The Sydney Symphony Orchestra plays in the Concert Hall for the builders.
**Why it matters:** the acoustic test before handover, i.e. inspection before practical completion (ABIC M2–M4).
*Source: D, M*

### Coburn curtains — 1973
John Coburn's tapestries hang as the stage curtains: *Curtain of the Sun* (Opera Theatre) and *Curtain of the Moon* (Drama Theatre).
**Why it matters:** the new architect interpreting the original architect's intent for colour. A moral-rights and authorship point for Part B (PC15).
*Source: N*

### First opera — 28 Sep 1973
The Australian Opera performs *War and Peace* in the Opera Theatre, almost a month before the official opening. The first public Concert Hall concert follows on 29 Sep 1973.
**Why it matters:** the building is in use before the official opening. In ABIC terms, the client takes possession before the practical completion notice (clause M8).
*Source: Q, D*

### Official opening — 20 Oct 1973
The Queen opens the building.
**Why it matters:** our practical completion certificate.
*Source: J, D*

### Architects' Certificate of Completion — 31 May 1975
The architects certify completion, after the 12-month defects period.
**Why it matters:** the real-world match for our SW-31 Final Certificate.
*Source: D*

### Grand Organ finished — 30 May 1979
Ronald Sharp's organ is completed, almost six years after the opening, costing A$1.2m against a A$400,000 budget. First recital 7 Jun 1979.
**Why it matters:** scope deliberately left out of the main contract to protect the completion date. It is not part of SW-31, and a clear cost-overrun example.
*Source: O, D*

## How this helps our file

- **Scenario 1 isn't affected**; it is 1961–62. But these events set the scene for **SW-31 (31 May 1975)**. By then the architects are Hall, Todd & Littlemore and the builder is Hornibrook. The group has to decide how the Utzon-era variation (SW-11, 1962) is carried through to a final certificate issued by the later team.
- **Part B examples beyond Scenario 1:**
  - client brief change after construction started (Mar 1967);
  - written professional advice from a consultant (Arup, 28 Mar 1967);
  - quality assurance by prototype (glass walls, 1969) and by test (Concert Hall, Dec 1972);
  - copyright and authorship (White Book; Coburn curtains interpreting Utzon's colour intent);
  - early occupation before completion (Sep 1973);
  - scope carved out of the main contract (the organ).
- These line up with the AACA competencies on the assessment page: **PC7** (communication and approvals), **PC15** (copyright, moral rights), **PC54** (site quality), **PC56** (defects, substitutions), **PC57** (variations, practical completion).
