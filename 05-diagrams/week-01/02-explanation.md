## Cluster-by-cluster

### Q1 — Profession vs business

- **What it means:** A profession is not just a skilled job. Beaton (via Cheetham & Chivers) lists traits like learned training, ethical code, altruistic orientation, self-regulation, and client/public focus. A business prioritises profit and market position.  
- **Why on diagram:** This is class question 1 and the conceptual entry point for Assessment 1.  
- **Links:** Feeds the core loop (ethics/altruism) and contrasts with the red tension box (profit pressure).

### Core loop — professional capacity

- **What it means:** Beaton defines professionalism as knowledge + skills + trustworthiness + altruism → **integrity**. Specialised knowledge creates **asymmetrical power** over the client; ethics and public duty balance that power. The Week 1 manifesto reframes this as **professional judgment** — synthesising fragmented knowledge when no procedure gives the “right” answer.  
- **Why on diagram:** This is the engine of the week — everything else connects here.  
- **Links:** Q2 obligations flow *from* judgment; Q3 power *shapes* who can exercise judgment; tension *limits* judgment when fees/scope shrink.

### Q2 — Beyond the paying client

- **What it means:** Manifesto point 3: future occupants, neighbours, communities, and future generations are affected but absent from the meeting. The “client not in the room” includes the child who will use a school in 2035, the future apartment owner, the person evacuating in a fire.  
- **Why on diagram:** Class question 2; shows *who* the profession serves.  
- **Links:** Depends upon professional judgment; contrasts with client’s immediate commercial preferences (Q5).

### Q3 — Who holds power?

- **What it means:** Multiple actors: paying clients and developers (fees, scope), practice owners (employment culture), ARBV (title, discipline), AACA (competency standards, accreditation), universities and institutes (education, advocacy), government/NCC (law). Week 1 also raises the **ARBV absorption proposal** — should architecture keep its own regulator?  
- **Why on diagram:** Class question 3 — who holds power and should they?  
- **Links:** ARBV/AACA enable the registration spine; client/practice power feeds the tension cluster.

### Q4 — Classic ↔ neo

- **What it means:** Beaton’s classic learned professions historically include divinity, law, medicine; architecture appears among **design professions**. Neo-professions proliferate as specialisation grows (IT, management, etc.). The question is whether architecture behaves like law/medicine (strong public duty, regulation) or is drifting toward commercialised neo-professional status.  
- **Why on diagram:** Class question 4 — your **position** goes here (mark on v02 hand-draw).  
- **Links:** Informs how strongly you draw the registration spine and public-interest loop.

### Tension — commercial vs professional (Q5)

- **What it means:** Manifesto point 8: reduced scope and fees cannot become a way to **withdraw judgment** while the public still expects it (Lacrosse Tower example in lecture). At student level: pressure for fast output, portfolio polish, or agreeing with the brief vs ethical, rigorous thinking. Manifesto point 6: innovation for company profit ≠ professional progress unless public benefit is redistributed.  
- **Why on diagram:** Class question 5; this is your critical edge for Assessment 1.  
- **Links:** Dashed line **limits** judgment in the core loop.

### Manifesto themes (lecture)

- **What it means:** Week 1 lecture argues registration should articulate **entrusted judgment**, not just competency; codes/contracts are **instruments** architects interpret; knowledge should be **collective**; the profession should be judged by **public contribution**, not only member success.  
- **Why on diagram:** Grounds PP1’s lens — not “how things are” only, but what the profession **should become**.  
- **Links:** Feeds core loop and Assessment 1 critical position.

- **What it means:** Assessment 1 requires a legible **Victoria pathway** as organising spine. This v01 shows education → experience/logbook → APE → ARBV → ongoing CPD/PI — details to verify in later weeks.  
- **Why on diagram:** Connects Week 1 theory to Assessment 1 deliverable.  
- **Links:** Green “Disciplinary Matrix” node; power cluster (ARBV, AACA) ties in here.

## Critical point (draft position for you to refine)

Architecture in Week 1 is framed as a profession defined by **entrusted judgment across fragmented knowledge**, not by exclusive technical knowledge alone. Power currently sits unevenly between **commercial clients/practices** and **regulatory/educational bodies**; the profession’s legitimacy depends on demonstrating **public interest**, not just title and competency. When commercial pressure reduces scope without reducing expected judgment, the argument for profession-specific regulation weakens — that tension should appear explicitly on your Assessment 1 matrix.
