# 03 — Presentation script: Week 01 — What is a Profession?

- **Paired diagram:** `01-diagram.md`
- **Time target:** 3–4 minutes
- **Tone:** spoken, clear

---

## Hook (20 sec)

“This diagram is my Week 1 map of **what makes architecture a profession** — not just a business — and how that connects to **Assessment 1’s disciplinary matrix**. I built it from Beaton, our class questions, and the Week 1 manifesto lecture.”

---

## Walk the diagram (2 min)

“**Start top-left, question one:** profession versus business. A profession is learned, ethical, and oriented to service and public duty — Beaton’s list via Cheetham and Chivers. A business is primarily profit and market driven. They can overlap, but professionalism fails when profit trumps altruism.

“**Move to the centre — the yellow loop.** Specialised knowledge gives asymmetrical **power** over the client. Ethics and altruism turn that into **trust**. The Week 1 manifesto puts **professional judgment** at the centre — not just ticking competencies, but synthesising conflicting knowledge when no procedure gives one right answer.

“**Right side, question two:** obligations **beyond the paying client**. Future occupants, neighbours, communities, future generations — the ‘client not in the room.’ The child who will use the school in 2035 was not at the client meeting.

“**Left, question three — power.** Clients and developers control fees and scope. ARBV and AACA control registration and standards. Universities and institutes shape education and culture. The lecture also raised whether ARBV should stay a standalone board or merge into a broader regulator — that’s a power question, not just admin.

“**Bottom-middle, question four:** classic professions like law and medicine versus **neo-profession** pressure. Beaton places architecture among design professions. My diagram leaves space for my position — I’m still deciding how far architecture sits on that spectrum.

“**Red box, question five — tension.** Reduced fees and scope cannot mean reduced **judgment** while the public still expects it. At student level, that might look like rushing studio output versus ethical, careful design thinking.

“**Manifesto cluster:** registration should express **entrusted judgment**; codes and contracts are **instruments** we interpret; knowledge should be **collective**; we should be measured by **public contribution**.

“**Bottom strip:** draft **Victoria registration spine** for Assessment 1 — education, experience, APE, ARBV, then CPD. I’ll add exact gateways and times as we learn them.”

---

## Critical takeaway (40 sec)

“The key insight for me is: architects are regulated because society expects **independent judgment across fragmented knowledge**, not because we alone hold secret technical knowledge. If we compete by withdrawing responsibility — or let commercial pressure shrink our role while judgment is still expected — the case for profession-specific regulation gets weaker. That tension is what I’ll keep developing toward Assessment 1.”

---
