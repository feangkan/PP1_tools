## 4. The overarching position (yours to finalise)

Working spine — *responsibility without authority*:

> Architecture makes individuals responsible — for the public who are not in the room, for compliance with rules they had no route to write, for a design they may no longer control, for conditions they cannot change alone — and then allows authority over each of those things to sit somewhere else. Where responsibility and authority sit together, the profession's mechanisms work and must be defended. Where they have been separated, that is where reform belongs.

It grows out of Assessment 1's own finding (the profession polices its visible gate tightly and its invisible ones loosely), which is what the rubric's "credible within the professional system you mapped in Assessment 1" rewards. Rewrite it in your voice — the rubric wants an *original* position, and wording is part of that.

## 6. Writing cards — one per point

Each card: your proposition (blank — write it), what the paragraph must establish (straight from the brief's tables), the evidence you already have, and the A1 cluster it grows from. Full detail for every point is in `02-manifesto-skeleton.md`; this is the writing-desk version.

**MAINTAIN/REFINE points must establish:** what currently exists (the actual mechanism) · who administers/enforces/funds it · why it matters · maintain or refine, with the specific refinement · what would be lost if it were weakened.

**CHANGE points must establish:** the current condition accurately, and why any existing mechanism is inadequate · who currently has responsibility or authority · what specifically should change · how the change could occur (actor + pathway, not "the profession should") · what changes as a result, including tensions.

**P1 — Registration.** Proposition: ________________________
Evidence: accredited education → ~3,300 supervised hrs / 35 NSCA competencies → APE (national paper + interview) → ARBV registration → CPD + PI insurance. The interview tests judgement; PI insurance is a public-protection instrument. Refine: supervision only means something if conditions permit it (→ P9) and the supervisor still has authority on the job (→ P8). *A1: Registration Pathway. PC1 competency lands here.*

**P2 — Duty to the public.** Proposition: ________________________
Evidence: *Architects Act 1991* ss.17–18; duty to withdraw rather than breach the Act. Read it as **standing**, not just obligation — the Act means refusing isn't merely losing a client. Extend to the people producing the building (Marshall, Week 7, L461–465). Beaton's test sits here: codes require conduct "beyond the personal moral obligations of an individual." *A1: Ethics & Public Value.*

**P3 — Independent review.** Proposition: ________________________
Evidence: Council planning + Building Surveyor sign-off gate every project; authority deliberately placed with someone other than the designer. Nobody checks their own work. One line on assisted documentation: a tool can produce an answer with confidence but cannot be accountable for it. *A1: Project Delivery Process.*

**P4 — The split apparatus.** Proposition: ________________________
Evidence: AIA advances / ARBV regulates — separate by design. Parlour, ArchiTeam, Architects Declare, ACA, UAW and the Wellbeing of Architects research fill what neither covers. Refine: resource them. Honest tension: the ACA co-produced the wellbeing research *and* argued at the Fair Work Commission against overtime protections — a body can research a problem and oppose the remedy. `[verify the ACA submissions directly]` *A1: Professional Bodies.*

**P5 — Codes + CPD.** Proposition: ________________________
Evidence: NCC/AS amendable through evidence and activism (Lui on egress and gender-inclusive bathrooms; Hill on the embodied-carbon roadmap) — the one place practitioners hold authority over the rules binding them. Refine CPD from compliance-ticking to formative judgement (Molander's epistemic measures), including how a fee is actually built — Marshall's point that architects often don't know. *A1: Research & Innovation + CPD.*

**P6 — Enclosed knowledge.** Proposition: ________________________
Evidence: Standards Australia progressively sold to SAI Global; advisory notes paywalled; flammable-cladding test data private. You are held responsible for complying with documents you must pay to read. Restoration, not invention — which is how you satisfy "don't propose a mechanism that already exists unless you explain why the existing one is inadequate." `[verify Standards Australia's 2026 ownership]` *A1: Research & Innovation.*

**P7 — Rule-making.** Proposition: ________________________
Evidence: entry policed tightly; code and standard amendment loosely gated — "amendable by whoever shows up." A small group sits across schools, Institute and Board at once. Fix the opacity, not the existence: published committee membership and interests, conflict disclosure, limits on cross-appointments. Name no individuals without evidence. *A1: Power Structure — your critical position, almost verbatim.*

**P8 — Procurement.** Proposition: ________________________
Evidence: novated D&C (AS4902 + novation) standard in Victorian government and commercial work 20+ years. AIA 2019 National Novation Survey: 266 practices, 484 projects, "an enormous opportunity to address building quality and prevent risk." Lost client contact, restricted site access, exclusion from value management. **Authority removed by contract alone, without loss of registration.** The voluntary Code/Deed of Novation are inadequate *because* voluntary → make the Deed a standard condition of government procurement. *A1: Procurement note — your second invisible gate.*

**P9 — Conditions.** Proposition: ________________________
Evidence: four of the Wellbeing of Architects' five areas describe how work is *organised*; only psychosocial safety is individual. 2023 research; average student anxiety at a clinical threshold; wellbeing scales with pay. UAW: 37% regularly work overtime; a 22% pay claim in a current Fair Work review; award overtime grey areas and the "better off overall" test absorbing unpaid overtime. Two good practices competing on fee cannot unilaterally stop. Australian precedent: the BLF green bans — collective power over what gets built, not only over pay. Marshall: *"you can't individually solve problems that are structural."* `[verify every figure]` *A1: Employment.*

**P10 — Where the value goes.** Proposition: ________________________
Evidence: ~2% fee on a large project; four-times-salary multiplier → ~25% of the billable rate returns to the person doing the work; ~20% target practice margin; developers need 20–30%. **Fee scales last applied in Australia in 1994**, ended as anti-competitive — so don't propose restoring them; propose transparency plus an enforced floor (→ P9). This is also where technology sits: efficiency gains from any tool should be a profession-level decision about where the benefit lands. `[verify the figures and the 1994 date]` *A1: Employment + procurement note.*
