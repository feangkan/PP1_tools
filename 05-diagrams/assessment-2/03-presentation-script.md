## Slide 2 — The position

**On the slide, one sentence:**
> Architecture makes individuals responsible — for the public not in the room, for rules they had no route to write, for a design they may no longer control, for conditions they cannot change alone — and then lets authority over each of those things sit somewhere else.

**Say (~40s):** My position is called *responsibility without authority*. The profession is very good at placing responsibility on an individual — registration does it, the Act does it, professional indemnity insurance does it. What it is much less good at is making sure that same person holds the authority to act on what they have been made responsible for. Where those two things sit together, the mechanism works. Where they have been pulled apart, that is where reform belongs.

## Slide 5 — The five to maintain or refine

**On the slide — one dense row per point, small font:**

**1 · Registration** — APE interview + ~3,300 supervised hrs + PI insurance. Maintain the interview and insurance; refine so supervision is real (needs Pts 8 & 9).

**2 · Duty to the public** — Act s.17–18, duty to withdraw. This is *standing*, not just obligation; extend it to the people producing the building.

**3 · Independent review** — Council + Building Surveyor sign-off, independent of the designer. The separation *is* the safeguard — nobody checks their own work.

**4 · Split apparatus** — AIA advances / ARBV regulates; Parlour, ArchiTeam, UAW fill the gaps. Maintain the split, refine by resourcing them. *(Tension: the ACA researched wellbeing but opposed overtime protections at Fair Work.)*

**5 · Codes + CPD** — NCC/AS amendable (Lui, Hill); CPD mandated only since ~2022. Maintain the amendment pathway; refine CPD into formative judgement.

**Say (~50s):** These are the mechanisms I think work. Registration joins responsibility and authority on purpose. Independent review is the interesting one — it deliberately puts authority with someone other than the designer, and that separation is the safeguard. Point 4's the one honest tension I keep in — the ACA doesn't share one interest with the bodies it sits alongside.

## Slide 6 — The five to change

**On the slide — one dense row per point, small font:**

**6 · Enclosed knowledge** — Standards Australia sold to SAI Global; cladding test data now private. Restore public access — restoration, not invention.

**7 · Rule-making** — entry gated tightly, code amendment gated loosely; overlapping board seats. Publish committee membership + interests; limit cross-appointments.

**8 · Procurement** — novated D&C, 20+ yrs standard; AIA 2019 survey (266 practices / 484 projects). Authority lost *by contract alone*; mandate a Deed of Novation in gov procurement.

**9 · Conditions** — 4 of 5 wellbeing areas are organisational, not individual; the fee race-to-the-bottom is structural. *"You can't individually solve problems that are structural"* — Marshall.

**10 · Where the value goes** — ~2% fee · ~25% of billable rate returns to the worker · fee scales ended 1994. Not a price floor — transparency + the conditions floor from Pt 9.

**Say (~50s):** And these are the separations — you keep the responsibility, someone else holds the authority. Standards Australia was sold, so compliance knowledge sits behind a paywall. Under novation you stay legally responsible for a design you no longer control. Points 9 and 10 are the two I've developed most this fortnight, and they connect — which is my next slide.

## Slide 7 — Point 9: conditions (the new ground)

**On the slide:** Wellbeing of Architects: **4 of 5 areas describe how work is organised** — only psychosocial safety is individual · the race to the bottom is structural — two good practices competing on fee cannot unilaterally stop · Australian precedent: the BLF green bans · *"you can't individually solve problems that are structural"* — Tom Marshall, UAW

**Say (~50s):** This is the point that didn't exist two weeks ago, and it's the one I want feedback on. Exhaustion and unpaid overtime get treated as individual problems — be more resilient, negotiate harder, or leave. But four of the five wellbeing areas are about how work is organised. My Australian precedent is the Builders Labourers Federation green bans — a union that started on site safety and ended up with authority over what got built. Some of these figures are still unverified and I've flagged them.

## Slide 8 — Why 8 and 9 are one argument

**On the slide:** Novation has resisted twenty years of advocacy · *"a pipe dream in architecture because no one has any power to ask for that"* — Tom Marshall, on getting design locked at novation or being paid for redocumentation · → Point 8 names the repair. Point 9 supplies the instrument.

**Say (~40s):** This is the connection that made the manifesto hold together rather than being ten separate issues. The Institute has argued about novation for twenty years with voluntary instruments and it hasn't moved. Marshall's line is that it's a pipe dream because nobody has the power to ask for it. Point 8 names the right repair; point 9 supplies the only instrument likely to deliver it.

## Slide 9 — Where AI sits

**On the slide:** A stance, not a point. A tool assists production but **cannot be held responsible** for the work — so the authority to decide stays with the person who is. Surfaces inside 3 (review), 6 (enclosure), 10 (where the gains land).

**Say (~30s):** I deliberately cut AI as a manifesto point. It was half my draft and it made the argument weaker, because "is AI good or bad" is an opinion piece and the brief rewards mechanisms. It's now a stance: a tool can assist the work but can't be held responsible for it, so authority stays with the person who can.
