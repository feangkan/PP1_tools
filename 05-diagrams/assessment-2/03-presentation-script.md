## Slide 2 — The position

**On the slide:**
> **My position is called responsibility without authority.**
>
> Architecture makes individuals responsible — for the public not in the room, for rules they had no route to write, for a design they may no longer control, for conditions they cannot change alone — and then lets authority over each of those things sit somewhere else.

**Say (~40s):** The profession is very good at placing responsibility on an individual — registration does it, the Act does it, professional indemnity insurance does it. What it is much less good at is making sure that same person holds the authority to act on what they have been made responsible for. Where those two things sit together, the mechanism works. Where they have been pulled apart, that is where reform belongs.

## Slide 5 — The five to maintain or refine

**On the slide — one heading + full paragraph per point, small font:**

**1 · Registration.** The clearest case where the two are deliberately joined: the registered architect is the one accountable and the one the law permits to act. The pathway — accredited education, ~3,300 supervised hours against 35 NSCA competencies, the APE with its national paper and interview, then ARBV registration with CPD and PI insurance — tests judgement rather than recall. The refinement: supervised experience only means anything if supervision is real, which depends on conditions (point 9) and on whether the supervisor still holds authority on the job (point 8).

**2 · Duty to the public.** Read ss.17–18 as standing, not merely obligation. The Act, backed by the Board, means that refusing to do something improper isn't simply an individual losing a client — it is a duty with institutional weight behind it. That is exactly Beaton's test, that professional codes demand conduct "beyond the personal moral obligations of an individual." Tom Marshall's Week 7 answer extends the same duty inward: as much as you care about the occupant you're designing for, what about the people producing the building?

**3 · Independent review.** Council planning and Building Surveyor sign-off deliberately place authority with someone other than the person responsible for the design. This is the one point where the spine's logic runs the other way and should — nobody checks their own work, and the separation is the safeguard. It also gives the cleanest line on assisted documentation: a tool produces an answer with confidence but cannot be accountable for it, so the second set of eyes matters more, not less.

**4 · The split apparatus.** The AIA advances the discipline, the ARBV regulates, and they are separate by design. Parlour, ArchiTeam, Architects Declare, the ACA and the UAW fill what neither covers — above all by turning private experience into public evidence, which is what the Wellbeing of Architects research does. The refinement is to recognise and resource them. The honest tension to write, rather than hide: these bodies hold evidence but not authority, and they don't share one interest — the ACA co-produced the wellbeing research and has argued at the Fair Work Commission against overtime protections.

**5 · Codes and CPD.** The NCC and Australian Standards remain amendable through evidence and activism — Lui on egress rules and gender-inclusive bathrooms, Hill on the embodied-carbon roadmap. This is the one place practitioners still hold authority over the rules that bind them, which is why it belongs in the maintain column. The refinement is CPD: registration assumes judgement, so CPD should build it rather than tick boxes — including, on Marshall's evidence, how a fee is actually built, because you cannot exercise authority over a system you can't read.

**Say (~40s):** Registration joins responsibility and authority on purpose. Independent review is the interesting one — it deliberately puts authority with someone other than the designer, and that separation is the safeguard. Point 4's the one honest tension I keep in, rather than hide.

## Slide 6 — The five to change

**On the slide — one heading + full paragraph per point, small font:**

**6 · Enclosed knowledge.** Standards Australia was progressively sold to SAI Global; advisory notes became paywalled; flammable-cladding performance-test data is now private. You are held responsible for complying with documents you must pay to read. Because a national standards body once worked as a public good and was hollowed out, this is restoration, not invention — which is how you satisfy the brief's instruction not to propose a mechanism that already exists unless you explain why the existing one is inadequate.

**7 · Rule-making.** Entry to the profession is policed tightly; amendment of the codes is loosely gated — amendable, in effect, by whoever shows up — with a small group sitting across schools, the Institute and the Board simultaneously. You are bound by, and responsible for, rules you had no visible route to influence. The change is to the opacity and the concentration, not to the existence of the committees: publish membership and interests, disclose conflicts, limit cross-appointments.

**8 · Procurement.** Novated design-and-construct has been standard in Victorian government and commercial work for over twenty years. The Institute's own 2019 National Novation Survey — 266 practices, 484 projects — found "an enormous opportunity to address building quality and prevent risk," and identified the mechanisms: lost contact with the original client, restricted site access, exclusion from value management. The architect stays professionally and legally responsible for the design while losing authority over it, by contract alone, without losing registration. The voluntary Code and Deed of Novation are inadequate precisely because they are voluntary, so the move is to make a strengthened Deed a standard condition of government procurement.

**9 · Conditions.** The profession treats exhaustion, unpaid overtime and low pay as individual problems — individual resilience, individual negotiation, or individual escape into development, project management or out of architecture altogether. But of the Wellbeing of Architects' five areas — value and worth, time and money, trust and transparency, risk and responsibility, psychosocial safety — only the last is about the individual; the other four describe how work is organised. And the race to the bottom is structural: two good practices bidding for the same project cannot unilaterally stop cutting fees, because neither controls what the other charges. The Australian precedent is the Builders Labourers Federation green bans: a union that organised first against deaths on site and then exercised authority over what got built.

**10 · Where the value goes.** A typical architectural fee on a large project is around 2% of construction cost; the four-times-salary multiplier means roughly a quarter of a billable rate returns to the person doing the work; developers won't build below a 20–30% margin. And the profession's own instrument for price is gone — fee scales last applied in Australia in 1994, abolished as anti-competitive. Don't propose restoring fee scales; propose transparency instead — a client who can't see how a fee was built can't tell a lean bid from an undeliverable one — resting on the enforced conditions floor from point 9.

**Say (~40s):** You keep the responsibility, someone else holds the authority — every one of these five reads the same way. Points 9 and 10 are the two I've developed most this fortnight, and they connect, which is my next slide.

## Slide 8 — Why 8 and 9 are one argument

**On the slide:** Novation has resisted twenty years of advocacy through voluntary instruments. Marshall's account of why is blunt — locking the design at novation, or even being paid for the redocumentation a variation causes, is *"a pipe dream in architecture because no one has any power to ask for that."* Point 8 names the right repair; point 9 supplies the only instrument likely to deliver it.

Other dependencies: registration (1) assumes conditions (9) and authority on site (8) · the duty to the public (2) is what the design-authority claim (8) rests on · an open amendment pathway (5) feeding a paywalled standard (6) is half a mechanism · paywalled standards (6) and invisible committees (7) are the same gate · transparency over fees (10) changes nothing without an enforced floor (9).

**Say (~40s):** This is the single clearest piece of evidence that my points need each other, not just sit beside each other.
