## LEGISLATION — blue — markers L1–L12

**Zoom-out:** The Act and the NCC are instruments — they enable buildings, and they can be rewritten.

**Ride:** START when the Act gives legal form → gate on who may use the title → withdraw rather than breach (sent back, not a fudge) → NCC as a building gate → courts if you fail the Act → loop: written → interpreted → amended. History (RIBA, Victoria register, London 1666) sits off the main ride as evidence, not as extra stages.

| ID | Short label | Typed content |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | START: Act gives legal form | The Architects Act gives legal form to knowledge, authority and responsibility — not consumer protection only. |
| L2 | Gate: who may use the title | Purpose of the Act: regulate conduct, handle complaints, regulate who may use *architect*, *architectural services*, *design*. |
| L3 | Cannot sign undone work | Autonomy clause: no signing, approving or supervising work you did not actually do. |
| L4 | Resign rather than breach — withdraw, not fudge | If continuing the job would break the Act, withdraw — even if you would still be paid. That is the “sent back, not ejected” move while you still hold the title. |
| L5 | Fire-isolated stair — public over payer | A client who wants a non-compliant substitution does not outrank the public. Explain, refuse, withdraw if needed. |
| L6 | Gate: NCC class and type | Buildings classified by use (Class 2, 3, 5, 6, 9…). Class plus storeys sets Type A, B or C construction. Room heights, light and air are turned into numbers (for example 2.4 m rooms; openable area versus floor area). |
| L7 | Fail analog: professional vs prohibited courts | Registered breach → Board / Tribunal. Unregistered person claiming the title → Magistrates’ Court. |
| L8 | Loop: written → interpreted → amended | Not optional. Not fixed. Repeal sits at the far end of the same line. |
| L9 | Subject, interpreter, co-author | What must I do? Why does this rule exist? Who may change its assumptions? Architects can move along that line. |
| L10 | RIBA was not a licence | Royal Institute of British Architects, 1834 / charter 1837: advance knowledge. First Silver Medal (1836) went to an essay on concrete, not a building. |
| L11 | Victoria public register 1922/23 | Statutory registration debated from 1887; enacted 1922 or 1923 (*do not lock the year*). Only twelve of the first thirty-three registrants were institute members. Registration is not a membership badge. |
| L12 | London 1666 — same instrument logic | Rebuilding Act and Fire Disputes Act: types of house, measurable light and air, rebuild-by dates, party walls, who decides a dispute, private land reorganised for the city. |

## BODIES — purple — markers B1–B8

**Zoom-out:** Institute advances the discipline; the Board holds it accountable; awards manufacture “good.”

**Ride:** two gates, not one. Gate A (AIA) is voluntary and **cannot** eject you from the title. Gate B (ARBV) is statutory and **can**. Awards are a third, non-legal “good” gate. Missing this split is what turned Bodies into a club list.

| ID | Short label | Typed content |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | Gate A: AIA is voluntary — cannot eject from the title | Architects act together and advance the discipline. Code of Professional Conduct (adopted 2006). |
| B2 | Gate B: ARBV is statutory — can eject | Society holds architects to a legal minimum. The Board regulates conduct, not design taste. |
| B3 | AACA sets the national test | National Standard of Competency; coordinates state and territory boards. |
| B4 | Network filling AIA gaps | ArchiTeam (small-practice insurance) · Architects Declare (climate) · Association of Architecture Schools of Australasia · Parlour (gender equity: about 21% of registered architects are women, about 40% of graduates) · Australian Consulting Architects. |
| B5 | Fragmentation is the problem | Australia does not lack institutions. Education, regulation, research, employment and public purpose do not sit in one body. |
| B6 | Awards write 'good' — not a legal gate | Categories decide in advance what counts (type, authorship, polish) and what stays quiet (social benefit, country, maintenance). In 1969 Southland Shopping Centre beat the National Gallery of Victoria under no-category judging; later taste reversed that. |
| B7 | Outside juries disagree | Social Impact Award uses non-architect jurors. Learning Environments Australia rarely picks the same school as the Institute education award. A separate Indigenous category is easier than putting Indigenous knowledge in every criterion. |
| B8 | Six media channels | Awards · journals · global design websites · practice PR · Instagram / TikTok · LinkedIn. Each rewards something different. Popularity can stand in for value. |

## ETHICS / PUBLIC VALUE — rose — markers H1–H8

**Zoom-out:** Duty runs past the fee-payer; “good architect” is a degree, not a kind.

**Ride:** START when specialised knowledge becomes power → ethics answers that power → duty past the fee-payer → loop: collective test back into knowledge. Fail analog: profit trumps service (professionalism fails) — that is not the same as a Tribunal ejection.

| ID | Short label | Typed content |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | START: knowledge becomes power | Specialised knowledge → asymmetrical power → ethics and altruism → trust → professional judgment → public interest, then back into knowledge. |
| H2 | Stage: ethics answers that power | The next station after power is duty, not a slogan. |
| H3 | Client not in the room | Future occupants, neighbours, communities, future generations. The child in a school in 2035. The person on a fire stair. |
| H4 | Gate: profession is not a business | Altruism, ethics, self-regulation, public duty. A business can be skilled and still put profit first. Fair reward is acceptable; professionalism fails when profit trumps service. |
| H5 | Fees do not shrink judgment | Reduced scope and fees cannot be a way to withdraw judgment while the public still expects it. |
| H6 | No good or bad architects — only degree | Loos, 1910: before God there are no good or bad architects — only degree. A villa can desecrate a lake either way. The Act then sets one minimum (good character, two years). What are awards measuring after that? |
| H7 | Searchlight or lantern | A professional magazine can light only what is photogenic and miss housing, climate, country, labour, procurement, public trust. |
| H8 | Loop: collective test — back into knowledge | Does this award, article, or campaign make architecture more useful and accountable — or only one architect more marketable? Apply the same test to the Tribunal, to CPD, and to a novation clause. |

## PROCUREMENT — navy — markers P1–P5

**Zoom-out:** A registered architect can lose design control by contract alone.  
**Tag on the sheet:** independent research — not from the lectures (the lectures name “procurement” once).

**Ride:** START with traditional AS4000 (keep design) → D&C shrinks the role → novation transfers the architect → authorship gone. This is **not** ejection from the park: the title can stay perfect. That is the contrast with Registration’s red box.

| ID | Short label | Typed content |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | START: traditional AS4000 — keep design | Not only hiring an architect. Traditional design–bid–build (AS4000): architect keeps design and administers for the client. |
| P2 | Stage: design and construct AS4902 | Contractor takes design obligations from a client brief. Architect’s role shrinks. |
| P3 | Stage: novated D and C | The client’s architect is transferred to the contractor mid-project. The contractual client becomes the builder. |
| P4 | Evidence: Institute survey 2019 | 266 practices, 484 novated projects. The Institute itself found a large chance to protect quality and reduce risk — and that novation can weaken design intent. |
| P5 | Not ejected: title intact, authorship gone | Direct talk with the original client · site access · value-management decisions. Registration status can stay perfect. Authorship does not. |

## RESEARCH / INNOVATION — teal — markers N1–N6

**Zoom-out:** Codes and performance change what architecture is allowed to be.

**Ride:** START at fit-for-purpose vs performance → codes are co-authored → amendment cases (bathrooms, carbon) → yesterday’s option becomes today’s gate → loop: one good makes another problem. Research is an amendment path into Legislation, not a separate island.

| ID | Short label | Typed content |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | START: fit for purpose, then performance | What a building *is* (use, construction, drawings) is not the same as what it *does* (landscape, carbon, moisture, care). |
| N2 | Codes are co-authored — not a closed file | Building rules change through activism as well as expert committees: fire egress after disaster, disability access, removal of segregationist plumbing rules, gender-inclusive bathrooms. |
| N3 | Stage: bathrooms into the NCC | Proposal 2023 → consultation 2024 → National Construction Code 2025. All-gender bathrooms adopted as a Deemed-to-Satisfy option (May 2026 on the lecture slide). |
| N4 | Stage: carbon written as a limit | Building about 200,000 homes a year at current practice would use about twice Australia’s emissions budget. A proposed cascade in the National Construction Code: about 461.8 down to 6.63 kg of carbon dioxide equivalent per square metre per year by 2028. Re-check the article before locking the numbers. |
| N5 | Gate: optional becomes ordinary | Livable housing: a Class 1a dwelling should be easy to enter and adapt; a continuous step-free path, with exemptions. Yesterday’s exemplar is today’s minimum. |
| N6 | Loop: one good makes another problem | Energy-efficient tight envelopes can trap moisture if they are not ventilated. A new rule answers the last rule. |

## POWER — terracotta — markers K1–K6

**Zoom-out:** A small overlapping group sits across school, Institute, and Board.  
Draw this **smaller** than Registration. That size *is* the argument.

**Why this group has no START → Stage ride:** Power is not a pathway. It is the overlap sitting *across* the other gates. Tight title / loose code and contract is the contrast with Registration’s linear ride.

| ID | Short label | Typed content |
|---|---|---|
| K1 | Not a pathway: interlocking seats | The same few people can sit on school leadership, the Institute, and the registration board at once. |
| K2 | Influence sits in relations | Client, builder, architect, regulator, and finance all pull. Power is not one throne. Bodies also renew every few years. |
| K3 | Tight title, loose instrument | Five requirements, three pathways, three exam parts for the name. Who amends the code, and who keeps the job after novation, are much more open. |
| K4 | Crisis without revolution | Defects and cladding accumulate. Most people in the room called it a crisis; few called it a revolution. Those already in post have little reason to force the shift. |
| K5 | Institution versus the public | The “good” can be assumed to be whatever is already written into the planning scheme and the code. That is not the same as the will of the people. |
| K6 | Change: who writes rules / keeps authorship | Point the profession’s energy at the two quieter gates: who writes the baseline rules, and who keeps authorship in delivery. That is how architecture better serves the public — not by polishing the title alone. |
