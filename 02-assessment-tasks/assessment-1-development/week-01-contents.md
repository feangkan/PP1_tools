Week 1 defines a profession as specialised knowledge used with trustworthiness and altruism, centres **professional judgment**, and hangs a first Victoria registration strip on Assessment 1.

---

## Topic / lecture frame

Professionalism in architecture: what distinguishes a profession from a business, who holds power, and where commercial pressure meets professional obligation.

**Manifesto live issue:** proposal to absorb ARBV into a broader business-and-professions regulator. The subject’s question is not “architects deserve their own board” but *what distinctive public capacity is profession-specific regulation meant to create?*

### Q1 — Profession vs business

| Profession | Business / trade |
|---|---|
| Altruism + ethics | Profit primary |
| Learned + specialised | Market / member focus |
| Self-regulatory body | Protects members’ market |

Beaton (via Cheetham & Chivers, p. 20): **11 traits** — status, professional body, learned, altruistic, autonomy, ethical code, non-commercial, collective influence, self-regulatory, collegial, client-focused.

Green theory box: *Profit OK — not if it trumps altruism.*

## Critical point (for A1, not a quote to paste)

Architecture is framed as **entrusted judgment across fragmented knowledge**. Power sits unevenly between commercial clients/practices and regulators. If scope/fees shrink but the public still expects judgment, the case for a profession-specific board weakens.
