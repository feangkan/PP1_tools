## REGISTRATION — gold — markers R1–R12

**Zoom-out:** The one compulsory gate — education, hours, exam, title, then a loop.

| ID | Short label (3–8 words) | Typed content to letter |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Accredited education first | Part 1 / Part 2 accredited course (AQF). Without this, the later gates do not open. |
| R2 | Five statutory requirements | Fit and proper (probity — criminal history, insolvency — not physical fitness) · prescribed study · two years’ practice · professional indemnity insurance · fees. |
| R3 | Two classes of registration | Practising and non-practising. Title is not one flat status. |
| R4 | Three pathways in | Architectural Practice Examination · Experienced Practitioner Assessment (portfolio; hard; often overseas) · Mutual Recognition (interstate/overseas; skips re-proving experience). |
| R5 | Logbook 3,300 hours | Thirty-five performance criteria. Maximum 1,650 hours before graduation; same cap for overseas hours; at least twelve months Australian after graduation. |
| R6 | Four competency groups | Practice management and conduct · project initiation and concept · detailed design and documentation · design delivery and construction-phase services. |
| R7 | Statement of experience | About 2,000–3,000 words; typically four projects; CV and PC report sit outside the count. |
| R8 | National exam | 120 minutes, 80 multiple-choice, closed book, National Construction Code–focused. |
| R9 | Interview probes gaps | 45–60 minutes. Hypotheticals on what you did *not* log. Breadth beats the “coolest” project. |
| R10 | Pass is not registration | After all three APE parts you still apply to the Architects Registration Board of Victoria. |
| R11 | Fail repeats the stage | Incomplete hours or a failed paper send you back to that stage — not out of the profession. |
| R12 | CPD and insurance loop | After the title: continuing professional development (mandated about 2022) and professional indemnity / public liability. Serious breach (Tribunal, public discipline register) can exclude you. |

**Entrance signs (tutorial board — letter at the start of this trunk):**  
Trust = autonomy · discretion · accountability.  
Risk is owed to the public and to the profession; more knowledge and experience means more risk you are trusted to carry.

## EDUCATION — green — markers E1–E6

**Zoom-out:** Judgment is trained, then kept alive — not a one-off competency stamp.

| ID | Short label | Typed content |
|---|---|---|
| E1 | University under AQF | Accredited higher learning. The university privileges inquiry; practice may not rank that first. |
| E2 | Judgment over competency | Registration should name the judgment society entrusts, not a checklist alone. Knowledge is fragmented; the architect synthesises. |
| E3 | Integrity = head heart hand | Professionalism is knowledge and skill plus trustworthiness and altruism. Specialised knowledge creates asymmetrical power over the client. |
| E4 | CPD is formative | Continuing professional development builds the quality of reasoning *before* the next judgment — not only after a breach. |
| E5 | Knowledge should be collective | Codes and contracts are instruments to interpret, not only barriers. Share useful knowledge; do not treat it as firm property only. |
| E6 | Classic or neo-profession | Architecture sits with the design professions. Classic learned trio is divinity, law, medicine. Commercial drift pulls toward a neo-profession. Mark *your* position. |

## LEGISLATION — blue — markers L1–L12

**Zoom-out:** The Act and the NCC are instruments — they enable buildings, and they can be rewritten.

| ID | Short label | Typed content |
|---|---|---|
| L1 | Act gives legal form | The Architects Act gives legal form to knowledge, authority and responsibility — not consumer protection only. |
| L2 | RIBA was not a licence | Royal Institute of British Architects, 1834 / charter 1837: advance knowledge. First Silver Medal (1836) went to an essay on concrete, not a building. |
| L3 | Victoria’s public register | Statutory registration debated from 1887; enacted 1922 or 1923 (*do not lock the year*). Only twelve of the first thirty-three registrants were institute members. Registration is not a membership badge. |
| L4 | Purpose of the Act | Regulate conduct, handle complaints, regulate who may use *architect*, *architectural services*, *design*. |
| L5 | Cannot sign what you did not do | Autonomy clause: no signing, approving or supervising work you did not actually do. |
| L6 | Resign rather than breach | If continuing the job would break the Act, withdraw — even if you would still be paid. |
| L7 | Fire-isolated stair | A client who wants a non-compliant substitution does not outrank the public. Explain, refuse, withdraw if needed. |
| L8 | Professional vs prohibited | Registered breach → Board / Tribunal. Unregistered person claiming the title → Magistrates’ Court. |
| L9 | NCC class and type | Buildings classified by use (Class 2, 3, 5, 6, 9…). Class plus storeys sets Type A, B or C construction. Room heights, light and air are turned into numbers (for example 2.4 m rooms; openable area versus floor area). |
| L10 | London 1666 same logic | Rebuilding Act and Fire Disputes Act: types of house, measurable light and air, rebuild-by dates, party walls, who decides a dispute, private land reorganised for the city. |
| L11 | Law is provisional | Written → interpreted → amended → repealed. Not optional. Not fixed. |
| L12 | Subject, interpreter, co-author | What must I do? Why does this rule exist? Who may change its assumptions? Architects can move along that line. |

## BODIES — purple — markers B1–B8

**Zoom-out:** Institute advances the discipline; the Board holds it accountable; awards manufacture “good.”

| ID | Short label | Typed content |
|---|---|---|
| B1 | AIA is voluntary | Architects act together and advance the discipline. Code of Professional Conduct (adopted 2006). |
| B2 | ARBV is statutory | Society holds architects to a legal minimum. The Board regulates conduct, not design taste. |
| B3 | AACA sets the national test | National Standard of Competency; coordinates state and territory boards. |
| B4 | The network around the AIA | ArchiTeam (small-practice insurance) · Architects Declare (climate) · Association of Architecture Schools of Australasia · Parlour (gender equity: about 21% of registered architects are women, about 40% of graduates) · Australian Consulting Architects. |
| B5 | Fragmentation is the problem | Australia does not lack institutions. Education, regulation, research, employment and public purpose do not sit in one body. |
| B6 | Awards write the value system | Categories decide in advance what counts (type, authorship, polish) and what stays quiet (social benefit, country, maintenance). In 1969 Southland Shopping Centre beat the National Gallery of Victoria under no-category judging; later taste reversed that. |
| B7 | Outside juries disagree | Social Impact Award uses non-architect jurors. Learning Environments Australia rarely picks the same school as the Institute education award. A separate Indigenous category is easier than putting Indigenous knowledge in every criterion. |
| B8 | Six media channels | Awards · journals · global design websites · practice PR · Instagram / TikTok · LinkedIn. Each rewards something different. Popularity can stand in for value. |

## ETHICS / PUBLIC VALUE — rose — markers H1–H8

**Zoom-out:** Duty runs past the fee-payer; “good architect” is a degree, not a kind.

| ID | Short label | Typed content |
|---|---|---|
| H1 | Knowledge becomes power | Specialised knowledge → asymmetrical power → ethics and altruism → trust → professional judgment → public interest, then back into knowledge. |
| H2 | Client not in the room | Future occupants, neighbours, communities, future generations. The child in a school in 2035. The person on a fire stair. |
| H3 | Profession is not a business | Altruism, ethics, self-regulation, public duty. A business can be skilled and still put profit first. Fair reward is acceptable; professionalism fails when profit trumps service. |
| H4 | Fees do not shrink judgment | Reduced scope and fees cannot be a way to withdraw judgment while the public still expects it. |
| H5 | No good or bad architects | Loos, 1910: before God there are no good or bad architects — only degree. A villa can desecrate a lake either way. The Act then sets one minimum (good character, two years). What are awards measuring after that? |
| H6 | Architecture, not Architects | The public sees individuals, not the value of the work. Recognition should educate and stay answerable to users, not build a clique. |
| H7 | Searchlight or lantern | A professional magazine can light only what is photogenic and miss housing, climate, country, labour, procurement, public trust. |
| H8 | Collective test | Does this award, article, or campaign make architecture more useful and accountable — or only one architect more marketable? Apply the same test to the Tribunal, to CPD, and to a novation clause. |

## PROCUREMENT — navy — markers P1–P5

**Zoom-out:** A registered architect can lose design control by contract alone.  
**Tag on the sheet:** independent research — not from the lectures (the lectures name “procurement” once).

| ID | Short label | Typed content |
|---|---|---|
| P1 | Procurement is the whole bringing-into-being | Not only hiring an architect. Traditional design–bid–build (AS4000): architect keeps design and administers for the client. |
| P2 | Design and construct | AS4902. Contractor takes design obligations from a client brief. Architect’s role shrinks. |
| P3 | Novated design and construct | The client’s architect is transferred to the contractor mid-project. The contractual client becomes the builder. |
| P4 | Institute survey, 2019 | 266 practices, 484 novated projects. The Institute itself found a large chance to protect quality and reduce risk — and that novation can weaken design intent. |
| P5 | What is lost after novation | Direct talk with the original client · site access · value-management decisions. Registration status can stay perfect. Authorship does not. |

## RESEARCH / INNOVATION — teal — markers N1–N6

**Zoom-out:** Codes and performance change what architecture is allowed to be.

| ID | Short label | Typed content |
|---|---|---|
| N1 | Fit for purpose, then performance | What a building *is* (use, construction, drawings) is not the same as what it *does* (landscape, carbon, moisture, care). |
| N2 | Codes are co-authored | Building rules change through activism as well as expert committees: fire egress after disaster, disability access, removal of segregationist plumbing rules, gender-inclusive bathrooms. |
| N3 | Bathrooms into the NCC | Proposal 2023 → consultation 2024 → National Construction Code 2025. All-gender bathrooms adopted as a Deemed-to-Satisfy option (May 2026 on the lecture slide). |
| N4 | Carbon written as a limit | Building about 200,000 homes a year at current practice would use about twice Australia’s emissions budget. A proposed cascade in the National Construction Code: about 461.8 down to 6.63 kg of carbon dioxide equivalent per square metre per year by 2028. Re-check the article before locking the numbers. |
| N5 | Optional becomes ordinary | Livable housing: a Class 1a dwelling should be easy to enter and adapt; a continuous step-free path, with exemptions. Yesterday’s exemplar is today’s minimum. |
| N6 | One good makes another problem | Energy-efficient tight envelopes can trap moisture if they are not ventilated. A new rule answers the last rule. |

## POWER — terracotta — markers K1–K6

**Zoom-out:** A small overlapping group sits across school, Institute, and Board.  
Draw this **smaller** than Registration. That size *is* the argument.

| ID | Short label | Typed content |
|---|---|---|
| K1 | Interlocking seats | The same few people can sit on school leadership, the Institute, and the registration board at once. |
| K2 | Influence sits in relations | Client, builder, architect, regulator, and finance all pull. Power is not one throne. Bodies also renew every few years. |
| K3 | Tight title, loose instrument | Five requirements, three pathways, three exam parts for the name. Who amends the code, and who keeps the job after novation, are much more open. |
| K4 | Crisis without revolution | Defects and cladding accumulate. Most people in the room called it a crisis; few called it a revolution. Those already in post have little reason to force the shift. |
| K5 | Institution versus the public | The “good” can be assumed to be whatever is already written into the planning scheme and the code. That is not the same as the will of the people. |
| K6 | What should change | Point the profession’s energy at the two quieter gates: who writes the baseline rules, and who keeps authorship in delivery. That is how architecture better serves the public — not by polishing the title alone. |
