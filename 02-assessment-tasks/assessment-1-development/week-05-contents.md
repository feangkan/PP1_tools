## One-sentence

Week 5 treats the Building Code / NCC as an **instrument of law** that enables buildings to exist: not a neutral manual, provisional (written → interpreted → amended → repealed), and a place where the architect is **subject**, then **interpreter**, then **co-author**.

## Lecture — Architecture and the Instruments of Law / The Building Code

### Core claim (closing slides)

instruments enable buildings to exist · they are not neutral · they are provisional — not optional · architects: subject → interpreter → co-author · we design buildings — and participate in shaping the instruments that produce them

Closing question: **Are we designing buildings—or instruments?**

### Three positions (draw as a small hierarchy)

| Role | Question on the slide |
|---|---|
| **Subject** | What must I do? / What does the instrument require me to do? |
| **Interpreter** | Why does this requirement exist? What problem is it trying to solve? What values does it contain? / How should I realise that intention? |
| **Co-author** | Who gets to participate in changing it? Are its assumptions still adequate? |

The Building Code as more than compliance: *how rules are made, what worlds they produce, and what agency architects have within and over them.*

### Today: NCC — measurable translations

| Clause / topic | Short label to draw |
|---|---|
| A6 Building classifications | Use class (2, 3, 5, 6, 9 …) + rise in storeys → Type A / B / C |
| C2D2 Type of construction | Type determines construction |
| F5D2 Heights of rooms | Many rooms 2.4 m; corridors/sanitary 2.1 m; assembly >100 people 2.7 m |
| F6D2–F6D3 Natural light | Light converted into a measurable requirement (slide marks 10% / 5%) |
| F6D6–F6D7 Ventilation | Openable area vs floor area; else mechanical |

Repeated lecture tags: buildings divided into types/rates · type determines construction · minimum internal dimensions · light / ventilation converted into measurable requirements.

### Standard vs bespoke (instrument of service)

- **Standard:** architect determines with the Building Surveyor; outcome prescribed  
- **Bespoke:** other options tested with subject experts (DDA consultants, façade engineer); solution interpreted and demonstrated  
- Slide question: *What if the Performance Requirement itself contains an assumption that needs to change?*  
- Cite on slide: Jacob Reidel & Elizabeth Christoforetti, “Instruments of Service: Building Code Battleground,” *Harvard Design Magazine* 52 (2023)

### Law is provisional

Written → interpreted → amended → repealed

### Cooke — can the Building Code be ethical?

John Cooke, “Architects and Engineers: Practising in the Public Interest,” *UNSW Law Journal* 14, no. 1 (1991): 73–101.

Slide paraphrase: the good is defined by the law — assumed written into the planning scheme and the building code; the politician writing these into existence expresses the will of the people. Tension: **institution of the law vs the will of the people**.

### London Act 1666 ↔ today’s NCC / planning (parallel columns)

**Then (1666):** Rebuilding of London Act + Fire of London Disputes Act — reconstruct the city as a functioning social, economic and physical system. Wren/Hooke Monument; coal tax; street widening / fire breaks; Wren geometric plan (not the one built). Lesser and Larger House; standardized housing (British History Online).

**Same four tags as NCC:** types/rates · type determines construction · minimum dimensions · measurable light and ventilation.

**Rules for a social system (not only construction):**

| Theme | 1666 | Today (slides) |
|---|---|---|
| TIME | Rebuild within prescribed periods (three years) | Planning permit expiry / commencement and completion — development rights conditional in time |
| NEIGHBOURS | Party walls, adjoining owners | Boundary / setback / amenity; VBA Part 7 / Building Act 1993 — protect neighbouring land |
| DISPUTES | Who decides when parties disagree | Institutions of interpretation and judgement |
| PROPERTY / CITY | Private land rights reorganised for reconstruction | Ownership ≠ unrestricted control; private property reorganised for collective reconstruction |

**The code constructs a system** (slide cluster):

- BUILDING — health / safety / amenity  
- CITY — streets / land / neighbours  
- ECONOMY — taxation / value / reconstruction (coal tax → chimneys; surveyor funded; drawings for approval → “the existence of the Architect was required”)  
- INSTITUTIONS — surveyors / disputes / responsibility  

*The Building Code is not merely a construction manual. It is a model of …* (sentence cuts on the slide).

### Who gets written into the Code? (lived path)

lived experience → advocacy → consultation → NCC change → future buildings  

Slide example: 2023 proposal → 2024 consultation → NCC 2025; *final NCC 2025 adopted all-gender bathrooms as a Deemed-to-Satisfy option in May 2026.* Inclusive signage by Simona Castricum.

Lui line on the slide: *Architects can move from subjects of regulation to participants in its authorship.*

### When does a value become a minimum standard?

**Livable housing** — what was exemplary/optional becomes ordinary regulatory expectation. NCC 2022: Class 1a should be easy to enter, navigate, adaptable; continuous step-free path subject to exemptions. (Image credit on slide: Swanbuild, 2024.)

### When one good produces another problem

energy efficiency → tighter envelope → moisture risk → new regulatory response  

(*Building Code Compliant in Principle / Unventilated, no exhaust fan running* — slide caption.)
