## Task in one sentence

Develop an original **A1 Disciplinary Matrix diagram** that explains how the architectural profession operates, why it exists, who it serves, and how it might evolve — with a **legible pathway to registration in Victoria** as the organising spine — plus a short critical reading note.

## What the diagram must do

1. Show a **pathway to registration** (Victoria): education → experience → exam/interview → registration, with timeframes, gateways, contingencies
2. Relate **education (incl. CPD)**, **employment**, **registration (+ insurance)**, **legislation**, **professional bodies**, **ethics / codes**, **procurement**, **research**, **public value**, **innovation**
3. Take a **critical position**: responsibility, power/influence, tensions/gaps, what may need to change, how architecture better serves society
4. Use relational language: *enables / limits / depends upon / influences / causes / prevents*
5. Evolve through **iteration** (not one polished poster at the end)



## Week 1 class questions (exact — from your slide)

1. What distinguishes a profession from a business, in their own words?
2. What obligations does an architect have beyond the paying client?
3. Who currently holds power over the architecture profession — and is that where it should sit?
4. Is architecture more like the "classic" professions (law, medicine) or more like a neo-profession? Why?
5. Where do they see tension between commercial pressure and professional obligation, even at student level?

## Extra critical prompts from Assessment 1 brief (not on the class slide)

- How is professional judgement developed?
- Who maintains professional standards?
- How does the profession continue to evolve?



## Method (4 stages)


| Stage | Action                                                                                           |
| ----- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| 1     | Quick hand sketch of current understanding (after Week 1) — messy OK                             |
| 2     | Choose / adapt a framework: pathways, ecosystems, layered systems, networks, cycles, hierarchies |
| 3     | After each lecture: reorganise (don’t only add) — redraw when structure fails                    |
| 4     | Refine through multiple versions → clearer, more integrated, more conceptual, more critical      |
