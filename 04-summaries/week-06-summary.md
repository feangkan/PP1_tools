## One-sentence takeaway

> Innovation is any shortening of the production process, and the professional question is never *can we shorten it* but **who captures the benefit** — the individual/practice/tech-company (private value) or the profession and public (shared value) — and AI is the sharpest current test of that question.

## Key points

1. **A "good architect" is not merely compliant** — they understand the system they work in and exercise judgment about when established practice is inadequate. Registration is a minimum threshold, not the profession's ambition.
2. **The "client not in the room"** — occupants, future users, the environment, the public — is who professional trust is ultimately owed to.
3. **Kuhn's cycle applies to practice:** anomalies in accepted practice (e.g. condensation failures) accumulate → research/inquiry/debate → evidence changes the NCC → the exception becomes normal practice. *The profession helps produce the knowledge through which rules change; it doesn't only obey them.*
4. **Innovation = shortening the production process** (fewer components, less time, less labour). The "research gap" closed is the benefit.
5. **Where the benefit goes** — three models: balanced cost/price; the **collective** model (open-sourced research → taught → standard practice → public benefit, e.g. a university open-sourcing medical research); the **entrepreneur** model (innovation kept as proprietary IP, savings not passed on, e.g. Apple).
6. **The machinery of a learning profession:** anomaly → inquiry → research/evidence → *shared outside the practice* → professional debate → publication → institutional adoption → education → standards/codes → normal practice. Sharing knowledge is how the discipline stays relevant, not just altruism.
7. **Privatisation of professional knowledge** is already advanced — Standards Australia sold to SAI Global (public advisory notes became paywalled IP; flammable-cladding test data now private), Revit/BIM licences embedding disciplinary knowledge firms must rent, paid green-building certification schemes. **When a method becomes a black box, professional agency drops** — architects become users who can't interrogate the assumptions.
8. **Easterling — design the profession, not just the buildings:** the growth suburb, the Port of Melbourne, the Shenzhen SEZ are shaped by rules, standards, contracts and incentives ("active forms") as much as by designers. "The action is the form." The profession is arguably responsible for designing the *systems that design buildings*.
9. **New descriptions of architecture** — ask what architecture *does* / how it *performs* / *who for*, not only how it's made (Norwegian tourist-route car parks specified by performance guidance and a surveyor's sticks, to protect trees, not by drawings).
10. **AI makes all of this concrete.** It shortens production dramatically = one form of innovation. But shortening ≠ progress. If a 10-hour task becomes 1 hour, does the benefit go to the client, practice, employee or tech company? And **AI gives answers without the disciplinary knowledge to judge them** — the gap between *confidence* and *professional judgment*.
11. **Gwyl Jahn's entrepreneur view:** technology isn't bad, but AI + capitalism concentrates power and removes individual choice via competition. Use AI as **leverage** on real problems (ideally ones many people have) — *know what needs doing + AI can do it = benefit*. Don't become a **reverse centaur** (AI thinks, you fetch). Never outsource thinking or learning. Value rises for wisdom/experience/agency; falls for technical skill and rote knowledge work.
12. **The graduate squeeze:** repetitive graduate tasks automate first; the experience ladder that lets you *know what needs doing* is being pulled up; expect unpaid internships to become the way disciplinary knowledge is acquired.
13. **Who checks AI output?** Current policy dumps responsibility on the individual (and the graduate being told to "go faster"). Helen's argument: checking must be *collective* — experienced people, a "fresh set of eyes," like an engineering documentation review. RMIT + ARBV are researching AI use in Australian practice now; results ~March–April 2027.

## Concepts & definitions

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| Innovation | Any shortening of the production process; the closed "research gap", expressed as a benefit |
| Private vs shared value | Who keeps the benefit of an innovation — individual/practice/shareholders vs profession/public |
| The collective (machinery) | The pathway by which one practitioner's anomaly becomes normal professional practice |
| Black box | A tool/method whose assumptions can't be interrogated; using it lowers professional agency |
| Active form / "the action is the form" | Easterling — the rule, protocol, incentive or org arrangement is the real design object, not the building |
| Reverse centaur | The bad human–AI configuration: AI does the thinking, the human just executes |
| Leverage (Jahn) | Applying AI to a real, widely-held problem — not to your own coursework |
| Confidence vs professional judgment | Being able to *generate* an answer vs being able to *judge* whether it's appropriate, correct, in the public interest |
