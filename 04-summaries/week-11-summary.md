## One-sentence takeaway

> The Simple Works Contract turns every change on site into a timed chain of written requests and responses (instruction, claim, assessment, certificate), with the architect acting both as the owner's agent and as a fair certifier between owner and builder.

## 1. Contracts as an instrument (SW slides 2 and 71; Forms slide 1)

- Helen reuses the Week 10 values and ties each one to a feature of the contract:

  | Value | What the contract does |
  |---|---|
  | **Certainty** | A formal process keeps every party able to finish the project; ABIC requires claims to be presented and resolved on time |
  | **Transparency** | The process is understood by all parties; documents are required and referred to throughout |
  | **Value** | Contract administration becomes clearer and less prone to dispute |
  | **Stability** | The contract is equitable: each risk is allocated to one party or the other |
  | **Interlinked and cyclical** | A request from one party needs a response from another, and each decision feeds back into the timeline and cost |

- **The architect has two roles:**
  1. **The client's representative** (agent).
  2. **Certifier, assessor and approver**, who must act with judgement, integrity, fairness and impartiality (Architects Regulations 2015).

  This is the tension the ARBV letter can discuss: you are paid by the owner but must certify fairly to the builder.
- The recap slide adds two clauses: A6 (the architect administers the contract) and R5 (the contract benefits successors and assignees).

## 3. The clauses, section by section

| Section | Key rule | Time limit |
|---|---|---|
| **A Overview** | Cooperative contracting: act reasonably and cooperate. The contractor warrants proper work, good new materials and legal compliance. The owner warrants it has the money. **A7: every architect's instruction must be in writing** | Contractor begins work within **10 working days** of getting possession (A3)<br>**A8: dispute a certificate or decision within 20 working days**, or lose the right |
| **B Documents** | B1: anyone who finds a discrepancy gives written notice, and the architect resolves it by written instruction (copy to the owner). B2: order of precedence | "Promptly" |
| **C Security** | Cash retention is **up to 10% of each progress payment**, until it reaches the Item 8 % (default 5%). Or two unconditional guarantees (2.5% each). The owner holds retention in trust. **C6: 50% is released at practical completion**; the rest with the final certificate | Guarantees within 10 working days of signing |
| **D Liability** | The contractor carries the risk from possession of the site until **4 pm on the day of the practical completion notice**; then the owner does | — |
| **E Insurance** | Public liability, contract works, workers' compensation. The contractor claims first, unless the owner caused the loss | — |
| **F The site** | F1: the owner gives possession once the contract is signed, the insurances are in place and the permits are obtained. **F5: a latent condition** is a ground or site condition a competent contractor would not have anticipated | Possession within **10 working days**<br>Latent condition notified within **5 working days** |
| **G Building the works** | The contractor sets out, supervises and keeps work health and safety. **G7: the architect may instruct opening up or testing.** G8: the contractor can claim the cost only if the work turns out not to be defective | — |
| **H Claims to adjust the contract** | H1: notify promptly, then a detailed claim. H2: the claim needs a trade-by-trade breakdown, overheads and profit (Item 17), and the effect on time. **H4: the architect decides price and/or time together** | Claim within **20 working days**<br>**Architect's written decision within 20 working days** |
| **J Variations** | J1: written instruction, any time before practical completion. The contractor keeps building to the documents until told to proceed. J1.2: may ask for an estimate or quote. J1.4: a variation changes scope, dimension, materials or quality, detail, order or sequence. **J3: the architect instructs whether to proceed** (or to negotiate). J6–J8: when an official document forces a variation | Estimate/quote within **20 working days**<br>**Proceed / not proceed within 5 working days** of getting the information |
| **K Provisional / prime cost sums** | Adjusted to the actual cost plus the Item 19 margin. The architect may name a particular person to do the work | — |
| **L Adjustment of time** | **L1, delays with costs:** loss or damage; the owner late with possession; **an architect's instruction**; authorities; neighbour disputes; owner's consultants late with information; industrial unrest; suspension; owner breach or prevention. **L2, delays without costs:** weather or other causes beyond the Item 20/21 allowances. A claim is made under section H | **L3: notify within 2 working days** of a delay starting and ending |
| **M Completion** | **M1: practical completion** = in the architect's reasonable opinion, substantially complete with only minor defects; commissioning done; occupancy approvals obtained. M2: the contractor gives a defects list at least **10 working days** before expected completion. M4: the architect inspects and decides. M7: if the architect doesn't issue the notice, the contractor's date stands. M8: if the owner takes possession early, that counts as practical completion. **M9–M10: liquidated damages** are deducted from certificates if the works are late. M13: defects liability period | Architect inspects within **10 working days**<br>M7 default after **5 working days** |
| **N Payment** | **N1: the contract price is a lump sum.** N3: progress claims, monthly (option 1) or by stage (option 2: base, frame, lock-up, fixing). **N4: progress certificate.** N5: tax invoice. N6: pay within Item 10. **N11: final certificate** must state the GST, give reasons where it differs from the claim, and tell the owner to release the remaining security. N14: interest on late payment | Progress certificate within **10 business days**<br>Final certificate within **10 business days** of the final claim |
| **P Dispute resolution** | Keep performing (P1). A compulsory conference: written notice, then meet within **5 working days** (P2). Then other procedures | — |
| **Q Termination** | Owner: notice to remedy a substantial default within **10 working days**, then terminate (Q1–Q2). The contractor may terminate on the owner's insolvency (Q14). Q9: the architect certifies the final balance | — |
| **R Miscellaneous** | R1: documents are delivered by hand, mail, fax, or **email only if an email address is given in Schedule 1**. R6 severability; R7 waivers must be in writing | — |
| **S Definitions** | Latent condition, variation, working day (Mon–Fri excluding non-working days), and others | — |

## 4. The diagrams: one timeline, read four ways (SW 10, 11, 26; Forms 11, 14)

- **The base timeline:** tender and negotiation, then **site possession**, then progress stages, then **practical completion**, then the **defects liability period**, then the **final certificate**.
  - Example stage split: 10% possession, 15% base, 35% frame, 25% lock-up, then fixing.
  - The span from possession to practical completion is labelled the **contract administration period**.
- **Security (Items 7–9):**
  - A $1,000 project has 5% retention: 10% of each $100 monthly certificate is held until $50 is reached.
  - 50% is released at practical completion and the rest at the final certificate.
  - With two guarantees (2.5% each), one is given back at practical completion and one at the final certificate.
- **Insurance (Item 10):**
  - The architect carries professional indemnity and public liability throughout.
  - The builder carries public liability, contract works and workers' compensation until practical completion.
  - The owner carries public liability after practical completion. Builder's warranties vary.
- **Variations, extensions of time and price adjustments:**
  - A client letter asking for a change leads to the architect's variation.
  - The builder then asks for a claim to adjust the contract, an extension of time, or gives a quote.
  - The architect issues the instruction to proceed, the adjustment of time and the contract price adjustment.
  - The **old** practical completion date moves to a **new** one. If the work is still late, **liquidated damages** run from the new date.

## 5. The forms, in Helen's numbered order (Forms slides 10 and 13)

**Variation and extension of time:**

| Step | Who | Form |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Client | **Letter** requesting the change |
| 2 | Architect | **SW-12** Architect's instruction for a variation (J1) |
| 3 | Contractor | **SW-42** Details of claim: claim to adjust the contract, extension of time, quote (H, J) |
| 4 | Architect | **SW-13** Instruction to proceed / not proceed (J3) |
| 5 | Architect | **SW-11** Contract price adjustment (H4, J4) **and SW-14** Adjustment of time (L1, L2) |

**Payment and close-out:**

| Step | Who | Form |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Contractor | **SW-48** Progress claim summary sheet (N3) |
| 2 | Architect | **SW-30** Progress payment certificate (N4) |
| 3 | Contractor | **SW-49** Final claim summary sheet (N10) |
| 4 | Architect | **SW-31** Final certificate (N11) |

Close-out also involves:
- **SW-20** Notice of practical completion.
- **SW-33** Certificate releasing security.
- The contractor's SW-47, which notifies the architect that the works are at practical completion.

## 7. Site visits, substitutions, defects (video transcript and Forms slide 6)

- **"The project is a chain of evidence":** an instruction affects time, scope and cost, and each has consequences.
- **Order of precedence matters on site**, because the special conditions, specification and drawings describe the same thing in different ways.
- **Periodic site visits are quality assurance, not supervision.** The builder supervises. The architect checks general conformance of finished work. If something hidden looks wrong, the architect instructs it to be **opened up or tested** (G7). The contractor claims the cost only if the work was fine (G8), and must rectify it if not.
- **A substitution is never agreed on site.** It changes the description of the works, so the contractor requests it formally and the architect assesses quality, compliance, cost and time, then instructs through a variation.
- **Defects:** in the defects liability period the architect issues a defects list, and the builder fixes them before the final certificate.

## Concepts and definitions

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Lump-sum contract price | The builder's price covers everything needed, including provisional and prime cost sums (N1); it changes only through the contract's own mechanisms |
| Claim to adjust the contract | The contractor's formal request for more money and/or time (section H, form SW-42) |
| Variation | A change to scope, dimension, material or quality, detail, or order of the works (J1.4) |
| Instruction to proceed | The architect's written go-ahead for a priced variation (J3, SW-13) |
| Adjustment of time / extension of time | A new date for practical completion (L, SW-14) |
| Liquidated damages | A pre-agreed daily amount the owner deducts if the works finish late (M9) |
| Practical completion | Substantially complete, minor defects only, approvals in place; risk passes to the owner at 4 pm that day |
| Defects liability period | Default 12 months after practical completion; ends with the final certificate |
| Retention | Money held back as security (default 5%), half released at practical completion |
| Latent condition | A site condition a competent contractor could not have anticipated (F5) |
| Provisional / prime cost sum | An allowance for undescribed work / an unselected item, adjusted to actual cost |

## What this means for our A3 (file 5 and the forms)

These are checks against our current Register (`programme/register_v3.py`) and timeline. **Nothing has been changed yet.** They are for Kankawee Maksomboon and the group to decide.

1. **Our SW-31 and practical completion dates belong to the whole building, not our contract.**
   - Our SW 2018 contract is **Stage I (the podium)**, with a $9.6m contract sum and practical completion due 26.01.1963.
   - But our Register dates practical completion **20.10.1973** (the opening) and SW-31 **31.05.1975**, and quotes the whole-project $102m.
   - Under the contract, and in Helen's own Stage 2 example, the Stage I final certificate should follow Stage I practical completion (26.02.1963 after SW-14) plus the defects liability period (default 12 months), so **around early 1964**.
   - The $102m can stay as context, but not as the contract's final certificate figure.
2. **SW-11 is issued five months after SW-14.** Our Register has SW-14 in 11.1961 and SW-11 in 04.1962. Clause H4 makes the architect decide **price and time together, within 20 working days** of the claim (SW-42, 09.1961), and Helen puts SW-11 and SW-14 at the same step. Move SW-11 next to SW-14, or record why it was delayed.
3. **Step 1 is a client letter, and our variation comes from the engineer.** Our trigger is the engineer's advice (06.1961), which is fine. But the CAA (A.6 "assess and determine variations and obtain Client approvals in writing") means the Register should show the **client's written approval** of the podium variation before SW-12, next to our CAA D.8 row.
4. **SW-31 must show the arithmetic**, as in Helen's example:
   - original $9,600,000 + SW-11 adjustment **$5,000** = **$9,605,000**;
   - less retention held, less any liquidated damages, less previous certificates;
   - the amount due, in words;
   - **refer to SW-11** (the brief requires it).

   Decide the retention (default 5%) and liquidated damages (Item 24) in Schedule 1 so that SW-31 can use them.
5. **Schedule 1 links back to our other documents:**
