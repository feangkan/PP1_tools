# Summary: Beaton — Why Professionalism is Still Relevant (Week 1)

- **Source(s):** Beaton (2010); class discussion questions in `03-weekly-resources/week-01/`
- **Week:** 01
- **Date created:** 2026-07-26
- **Length target:** medium

## One-sentence takeaway

> Professionalism is specialised knowledge used with trustworthiness and altruism — power over clients that must serve people and the public good, not only profit.

## Key points

1. Classic professions began as divinity, law, and medicine; architecture appears among later “design professions,” while many **neo-professions** now claim similar status.
2. Beaton quotes Cheetham & Chivers’ **11 characteristics** of a profession (status, body, learned, altruistic, autonomy, ethics, non-commercial, influence, self-regulatory, collegial, client-focused).
3. What separates profession from trade/business is especially **altruism, ethics, and duty to those served and society** — not skill alone.
4. **Asymmetrical knowledge** creates professional power; ethics and trust are required to balance it.
5. Threats today include the internet, decline of self-employment, and eroded public trust — yet Beaton argues professionalism remains essential.

## Concepts & definitions

| Concept | Meaning |
|---------|---------|
| Professionalism | Knowledge + skills + trustworthiness + altruism → integrity |
| Asymmetrical knowledge | Professional knows more than the client → power |
| Neo-profession | Newer specialised occupation seeking professional recognition |
| Classic professions | Historically divinity, law, medicine (learned professions) |
| Altruism vs profit | Wanting fair reward is OK; professionalism fails when profit trumps service |
