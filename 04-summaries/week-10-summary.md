## Lecture 1: the CAA and Design Development

### 1. The law comes first: the Architects Code of Conduct (Architects Regulations 2015, Vic, Schedule 1)

- **Clause 4 (client agreements):** an architect **must not provide services without a written agreement**. The agreement must include: (a) the parties; (b) the architect's name, registration number and contact details; (c) the scope of services; (d) timeframes; (e) how fees are calculated; (f) estimates of disbursements; (g) how fees are paid; (h) how the client is kept informed; (i) how the client authorises the architect to proceed; (j) how a change to the services affects fees; (k) how the client authorises a change; (l) how the agreement may be varied; (m) the architect's right to withdraw; (n) how the agreement may be terminated.
- **Clause 5:** what it means to administer a building contract for a client.
- **Clause 9 (records):** keep the agreement, correspondence, financial records, client instructions, photos and drawings. Keep them for **10 years** after the services, or the Building Act 1993 s.134 limitation period, whichever is longer.
- **Clause 12 (conflicts of interest):** you must not enter an agreement where a conflict exists unless you disclose it.
- **The slides' main move:** every CAA clause is tagged with the Code of Conduct item it satisfies, shown as yellow "Architects Act + Regulation" labels. **The CAA is the Code of Conduct written as a contract.**

### 2. What a contract is

- A contract sets **the rules for a professional relationship**. Helen groups its parts under four values:
  - **Transparency:** a commitment to mutual communication.
  - **Value:** registration, professional indemnity and public liability insurance, and access to the profession (higher learning, regulation, altruism).
  - **Stability:** changes are inevitable and are part of the contract; it is not fixed; the possibility of termination and withdrawal.
  - **Certainty:** scope, time, cost, and how payment occurs.
- Offer plus acceptance makes an agreement. A contract records **the fixed elements, the ability for them to change, and the process for how they change**.
- Provocation: *"As architects no longer administer the full contract, what value are we still providing?"* If architects explained this value better, they could justify charging for it.

### 3. Professionalism as access to a system of knowledge

- **Doctor analogy:** a doctor gives the patient access to hospitals, specialists, medication, insurance and Medicare. An architect gives the client access to the building system:
  - **Law and regulation:** Architects Act, ARBV, Building Act, VBA, NCC, Australian Standards.
  - **Planning:** Planning and Environment Act, planning schemes, OVGA, DELWP/VPA.
  - **Market and finance:** ACCC, Fair Work, the bank and valuer, the title registry.
- **The CAA connects the architect, the client and that wider system.** The building contract (Simple Works) connects the client and the builder.
- **Each contract is an "instrument"**, an agreement that translates judgement into a recorded action. The chain is: professional judgement, then authorised, then communicated, then recorded action.

### 4. History of the CAA, through the Sydney Opera House

- Helen shows the **RAIA Year Book 1959–1960 "Form of Agreement between Client and Architect"**. It was a single page that covered **only brief/scope, cost and "the client will pay"**.
- **What it left unclear:**

  | Unclear item | What happened instead |
  |---|---|
  | Communication | Letters? |
  | Time | Renegotiate the contract |
  | Changes | Renegotiate the contract |
  | Termination and withdrawal | Court settlement |
  | How payment occurs | Renegotiate the contract |

  **"Hence the Sydney Opera House as a case study."** Our A3 asks what the modern CAA would have made explicit.
- **The ACCC determination:** the RAIA had to withdraw its fee-scale graphs (Fee guide no. 8 and the small projects fee guide) under competition law. That is why there is no mandatory fee scale today. Fees are negotiated and justified. (The year is not on the slide `[verify]`.)

### 5. The CAA 2024, section by section (as walked through on the slides)

| Section | Key point | A3 link (Sydney Opera House) |
|---|---|---|
| **A.1 Architect's obligations** | Reasonable skill, care and diligence; act as the client's agent; keep records and keep the client informed; **promptly tell the client in writing when the scope changes**; disclose conflicts; follow the Code; stay registered. Architects get a "fitness for purpose" exception under Australian Consumer Law. | The basis of our letter's promises |
| A.2 Not included | No warranty of fitness for purpose; no hazardous materials; the client maintains the building after completion | |
| **A.3 Cost of Works** | The forecast is indicative only. It is revised with advice, tenders and construction adjustments, and the client adjusts the Total Project Cost budget. | £3.5m forecast to $102m final cost |
| A.4 / A.5 / A.6 | The program is agreed and revised by agreement. Protracted services. **Suspended services: the architect is paid to the date of suspension.** | |
| **B Client obligations** | Appoint the architect as agent; issue instructions to the builder **only through the architect**; give prompt decisions; engage the specialist consultants in Item 4 | |
| **C Administration** | Named representatives; **either party may replace its representative but must notify the other (C.3)** | Cahill to Heffron (1959); Askin and Hughes (1965); Punch (1973) |
| D Fees | Monthly claims (D.2); payment within Item 7A (default 10 business days); interest per Item 7B (default 15%); **the architect may suspend if a payment is overdue (D.3.3)**; percentage, lump sum or time-based fees (D.4 to D.6); disbursements plus a 15% admin fee (D.7) | Hughes withholding Utzon's fees (1965–66) |
| **D.8 Change to the Services** | The architect must promptly notify how the change affects the fee. The parties then agree the new scope, fee and date in writing. If they can't agree, the architect may terminate with 10 business days' notice. | **Our Scenario 1 row (Jul 1961)** |
| E Specialist consultants | E.1: the client engages and pays; **the architect coordinates but is not responsible for their work**. E.2: additional consultants. | Ove Arup, Steensen & Varming |
| **F Copyright and moral rights** | The architect owns copyright; the client gets a **licence to use**, revocable if invoices are unpaid and irrevocable once everything is paid. F.2: the architect must be attributed as author (Item 5). | Utzon's design after he resigned; Hall, Todd and Littlemore |
| G Insurance and liability | PI and public liability insurance (Item 6); a liability cap; tort law and corporations law | |
| H General conditions | **Everything in writing (email counts)**; variations only in writing; **overseas mail is deemed received after 10 business days (H.8)**; figures in AUD excluding GST | Utzon's office was in Denmark |
| J Dispute resolution | Keep performing. Give notice, then **meet within 5 business days**, then mediation (Resolution Institute). Legal action only after that. | |
| **K Termination** | K.1: the architect may terminate for the client's breach (for example of B or D.3). **K.2: either party may terminate without reason on 30 business days' notice.** K.4: the consequences. | Utzon's resignation, 28 Feb 1966 |
| L / M | Special conditions (Schedule C) override the rest; definitions such as Cost of Works, Total Project Cost, The Design and Mobilisation Fee | |

### 6. Schedule A: scope of services

- **A, Core Architectural Service** (43 items): A.1 Concept Design, A.2 Design Development, A.3 Planning/Development Application, A.4 Construction Documentation. A.5 Contractor Selection and A.6 Contract Administration are covered "next week".
- **B, Additional Services** (37 items): B.1 Feasibility, B.2 Record documents, B.3 Planning negotiation, B.4 Illustration, B.5 Trade package documentation, B.6 Other.
- Helen colour-codes each line into **four kinds of work**:
  - Communication and approvals.
  - Coordination with consultants.
  - Judgement and assessment.
  - Producing drawings.

  **Drawings are only one part of the work.** The value is in judgement and coordination.

### 7. How services are charged (the fee map)

| Stage | Typical share of the fee |
|---|---|
| A.1 Concept Design | 15% |
| A.2 Design Development | 15–20% |
| A.4 Contract Documentation | 40–50% |
| Contract Administration | 30% |
| Planning application and contractor selection | Often charged as extra $ |

- The % fee items are the standard services, **and what we are typically insured for**. The extra-$ items are growing, so check that your insurance covers them.
- **Schedule B:**
  - Item 1: % or lump sum, per stage.
  - Item 2: time-based rates.
  - Item 3: disbursements. These are kept separate because they are not part of the description of the building and do not change through the contracts.
  - Item 4: specialist consultants (client or architect to engage).
  - Item 5: attribution.
  - Item 6: insurance.
  - Item 7A/7B: payment time and interest.
- **Schedule C:** special conditions.

### 8. Where is the value? Risk falls as cost becomes certain

- The project description and the cost firm up together:
  1. Concept: the design is in the minds of the architect and client.
  2. Design Development: the design is understood against wider planning aims.
  3. Documentation: the builder can understand the architecture.
  4. Construction: it becomes a complete building.
- The cost follows the same path:
  1. The QS's Cost Plans A, B, C and D (the predicted market).
  2. The tender price (a specific market price).
  3. Progress claims (the real price emerges on site).
  4. The final cost.
- **Stages are the points where the project description gets a cost value.**
- Indigenous engagement is shown as a parallel additional service: listening, relationship building, workshops, mapping knowledge, priorities, testing options, feedback, decisions, a changed brief.

## Lecture 2: Contract Documentation and Tendering

### 10. From uncertainty to commitment

- The same stage map, now marked **Tender issue** and **Issue for construction (IFC)**.
- The **contract documentation set** has four parts:
  - **Specification and schedules.**
  - **Drawings.**
  - Structural and civil engineer documents.
  - Services, other and early-engagement consultants.
- Example: Utzon's Sydney Opera House drawings (Utzon Archives).
- **Specification:** a written description, self-contained, organised in construction order. Each section states its scope and the Australian Standards it relies on. It sets **hold points and samples** for the architect and building surveyor to inspect, tolerances (the lowest applies; they are not cumulative), and substitutions (a request is a warranty that the substitute is equal, and it may be rejected).
- **Schedules:** materials and finishes codes (example: 305–309 Clarendon St, South Melbourne). **Drawings** link to the schedules through material codes and schedule codes.
- **Consultant coordination is the architect's job.** That covers:
  - Timely output from consultants.
  - Making sure consultants coordinate with each other.
  - Making sure everyone has the latest documents.
  - Scope changes inside fixed-price consultancies.
  - Losing a consultant.
  - **No clashes within the architecture.**
- **Programme:** stages as in the CAA, milestones and the **critical path**, which is the sequence of activities whose durations set the project length. As head consultant, the architect is responsible for timely documentation, including the consultants' documents, so the client does not lose time or money.
- **Cost plan example (Clarendon St, Cost Plan No. 1):**
  - Build-up: areas × rates, then design contingency 5%, construction contingency 5% and escalation, then consultant fees 10% and authority charges.
  - Totals: **total construction cost about $11.7m; total project cost about $13.0m.**
  - Exclusions: FF&E, ICT/AV, specialist equipment.
  - This shows the CAA's Cost of Works versus Total Project Cost split in practice.

### 11. From description to market commitment

- The architect is the **author**: copyright and intellectual property stay with the architect.
- The client gets a **licence to use** the design.
- The **competitive tender** turns the description into a market price.

### 12. Tendering is a professional and ethical process: AS 4120–1994 Code of Tendering

- **Ethics:**
  - Tender honestly and fairly.
  - **Only call tenders if you intend to proceed and the funding is arranged.**
  - Give equal information to every tenderer and enough time to tender.
  - No collusion, inflated "cover" prices or secret commissions.
  - Declare conflicts.
- **Principal's obligations:**
  - Complete tender documents with clear contract conditions.
  - State the selection criteria, the tender closing time and the validity period.
  - Answer **tender enquiries** (any answer goes to all tenderers).
  - Issue **addenda** (tenderers confirm receipt).
  - Keep tenders secure; late tenders are generally not accepted.
  - **Closing time:** not before 2 pm, not on a Monday or the day after a holiday, not in the Christmas shutdown.
- **Evaluation criteria:**
  - Pre-qualification and the Code of Practice.
  - Conformity with the tender documents.
  - **Value for money.**
  - Construction period.
  - Technical, managerial and financial resources.
  - Other commitments.
- **Negotiation** happens with one tenderer at a time. Unsuccessful tenderers are told the result.
- **Tenderers' obligations:** only tender if competent and intending to do the work; no improper conduct; confidentiality.

### 13. Tendering methods: different ways of structuring competition and risk

- The methods are open (public), selected, pre-registered, invited, negotiated, and **ECI** (early contractor involvement).
- The same five values reappear:

  | Value | What it means here |
  |---|---|
  | Innovation | The best price based on experience, workflow and building method |
  | Transparency | Public or selected tenders make the process visible and merit-based. Pre-selected tenders give fewer chances for builders to upskill. |
  | Value | The market prices the building and supply at that moment |
  | Stability | A flow of future projects lets the market plan |
  | Certainty | A check on the project's cost and buildability |

- **Comparing tenders (real example, Clarendon St, 16/09/2020, five tenderers):**
  - Price columns: as-opened price, value-management price, a **normalised price** (adjusted so tenders compare like for like) and the ECI fee.
  - Other columns: programme days, working week, key personnel, insurances, construction management plan, **addenda acknowledged**, methodology and tender validity.
  - The lowest as-opened price is not automatically ranked first, and one tender is flagged as an **outlier**.

### 14. The closing question (use it for every event in the A3 file)

> **From professional judgement to recorded action:** What changed? Who has authority to decide? Who needs to know? What are the consequences for scope, time, cost and quality? **What document needs to be issued, and what document records the decision?**

## Concepts and definitions

| Concept | Meaning |
|---|---|
| CAA 2024 | The Australian Institute of Architects' standard Client Architect Agreement: the Code of Conduct's required contents, written as a contract |
| Cost of Works vs Total Project Cost | Cost of Works = the building work the architect designs or specifies. Total Project Cost = that, plus fees, authority charges, FF&E, finance, contingencies and so on. The **client** manages the Total Project Cost budget. |
| D.8 Change to the Services | The architect's own "variation": notify the effect on the fee, then agree in writing |
| Licence to use | The client's right to use the design. Copyright stays with the architect. |
| Moral rights | The right to be attributed as author (F.2, Item 5) |
| Contract documentation | Specification, schedules and drawings, plus consultant documents; the basis for the tender and construction |
| Hold point | A stage where work stops until the architect or building surveyor inspects it |
| Critical path | The chain of activities whose durations set the project length |
| Normalised tender price | A price adjusted so different tenders compare on the same basis |
| AS 4120 | The Code of Tendering: ethical rules for principals and tenderers |
