## Learning goals

- Compare multiple organising frameworks (traits, continuum, apparatus, paradigm shift, divided line, structural/institutional invariance) for reading architecture as a system
- Distinguish structural qualities constitutive of professions from other expert occupations (Sciulli)
- Understand institutional consequences of professionalism in the wider social order (Sciulli)
- Connect Week 2 theory to Assessment 1 (disciplinary matrix + critical position), using the registration pathway as the organising spine

## Key terms

| Term | Definition (in your words) |
|------|----------------------------|
| Structural invariance | Qualities that define professions uniquely (vs other occupations) |
| Institutional invariance | Consequences professionalism introduces into social order / social change |
| Received wisdom (Sciulli challenges) | Professions ≈ other expert occupations; effects only socio-economic |
| Traits model | Five traits (disciplinary knowledge, regulation, autonomy, altruism, higher learning) placed around a central node |
| Continuum model | Occupation ↔ profession spectrum based on judgment vs repeatable competence (Goode and Moore) |
| Apparatus | Network of institutions/laws (ARBV, AACA, AIA, universities, Acts) exercising uneven power |
| Kuhn paradigm shift | Normal practice → anomaly → crisis → revolution → new paradigm |
| Divided Line | Plato's abstract (laws, knowledge) vs physical (building, labour) split, with drawings/contract as instruments on the line |
