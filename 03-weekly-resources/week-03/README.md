# Week 03

## Topic

The Architects Act 1991 and Architects Regulations 2026: why statutory regulation of architects exists, how it differs from voluntary institute membership, how registration (the APE pathway) works, and how the ARBV's compliance culture research reframes regulation as an ongoing professional mindset rather than a one-off gate. Pre-reading (Molander, Grimen & Eriksen 2012) supplies the theoretical vocabulary — discretion and accountability — that connects all three lecture parts.

## Learning goals

- Explain why the Architects Act exists beyond consumer protection, and how it differs from voluntary institute membership (RIBA/AIA vs ARBV-style statutory boards)
- Trace the registration pathway (APE: logbook/SOPE → National Examination Paper → interview) and the 5 statutory requirements for registration
- Define compliance culture and compliance mindset, and explain the "ripple effect" from professional identity to compliant outcomes
- Connect Molander et al.'s discretion/accountability framework (structural vs epistemic measures) to how the Act, CPD, and the Architects Tribunal actually work

## Key terms

| Term | Definition (in your words) |
|------|----------------------------|
| Statutory registration | Legal authority to practise derived from parliament (the Act), separate from and broader than voluntary institute membership |
| Professional vs prohibited conduct | Professional conduct = breach of the Code by a *registered* architect (ARBV disciplinary process); prohibited conduct = an *unregistered* person/body claiming to be an architect (prosecuted in the Magistrates' Court) |
| APE (Architectural Practice Examination) | The main pre-registration pathway: logbook + Statement of Practical Experience (Part 1), National Examination Paper (Part 2), interview (Part 3) |
| Compliance culture | The shared values, attitudes and habits within an organisation that guide behaviour, actions and decisions toward (or away from) compliance |
| Compliance mindset | An individual's internalised commitment to understanding obligations and doing the right thing even without oversight — reinforces, and is reinforced by, compliance culture |
| Overarching duties | The 7 duties (care, competence, honesty/integrity, legal compliance, confidentiality, impartiality, records/communication) owed to clients, the public, and the profession |
| Discretion (Molander et al.) | A professional's space to judge and act (structural) *and* the reasoning process used under uncertainty (epistemic) — architects' design judgment and conflict-of-interest calls are a form of this |
| Accountability measures | Structural (constrain the space of discretion — e.g. registration, tribunal) vs epistemic (improve the quality of reasoning — e.g. CPD, interview probing) — Molander et al.'s framework, applied to the ARBV system in the diagram pack |
