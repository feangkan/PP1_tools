## Topic

**The Good and the Bad Architect (part 2) + Innovation, Entrepreneurship and AI.**
Two paired lectures: Helen's professional/collective view of innovation (who captures the
value of innovation, and how knowledge becomes shared professional knowledge), and guest
Gwyl Jahn's ("Quill", Fologram / RunChat) entrepreneur's view of AI as leverage. Together
billed as **"The Entrepreneur + The Collective."**

## Learning goals

- Distinguish *individual/entrepreneurial* value capture from *collective/professional* value capture in innovation.
- Understand the machinery by which a practitioner's anomaly becomes normal professional practice (inquiry → research → publication → debate → institutional adoption → education → standards/codes).
- See AI as one form of innovation (it shortens production) and ask the professional question: **who captures the benefit?**
- Separate the *citizen's* concerns about AI (environment, extraction, data centres) from the *architect's* professional question (what will you use it for, who is accountable for checking it).
- Connect all of this to **Assessment 2**: which mechanisms currently work (maintain/refine) and which need to change.

## Key terms

| Term | Definition (in your words) |
|------|----------------------------|
| Innovation (Helen's definition) | *Any shortening of the production process* — the "research gap" closed, expressed as a benefit (less time, less labour, fewer components) |
| Value capture | Who keeps the benefit of an innovation — the individual/practice/shareholders (private value) or the profession/public (shared value) |
| The collective (machinery) | How a profession learns: anomaly → inquiry → research/evidence → shared outside the practice → professional debate → publication → institutional adoption → education → standards/codes → normal practice |
| Black box | A method/tool whose assumptions can't be interrogated; using it reduces professional agency (you become a user, not an author) |
| Active form / "the action is the form" | Easterling — the important design may be the rule, protocol, incentive or organisational arrangement, not the visible object |
| Reverse centaur | Gwyl — the bad outcome: AI does the thinking, the human just moves things around. The good outcome (centaur) is the human driving, AI as horsepower |
| Leverage (Gwyl's rule) | Use AI on a real problem you have (ideally one many people have), not on your own assignment; *"know what needs doing" + "AI can do it" = benefit* |
